#4750. Ripple Backs SEC's Crypto Regulation Proposal

Ripple has endorsed the U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission's (SEC) proposed 'Regulation Crypto Assets', calling on the U.S. regulator to massively change the way cryptos are regulated in the country.

On October 20, the blockchain firm sent a comment letter to the regulator, encouraging the SEC to go ahead with the proposed regulation. They believe that the regulatory uncertainty and policymaking through enforcement for many years have driven innovation in the crypto space abroad.

Ripple's Chief Legal Officer Stuart Alderoty shared the development on X. He highlighted that the SEC already has the legal authority to go ahead with the proposed regulation.

"We are for this proposal. The SEC clearly has the authority to do it," Alderoty wrote.

He also voiced concerns about the 'case-by-case' approach of 'coin-by-coin' enforcement that the regulator had used for so long. Ripple, therefore, says there is a need for a clear set of rules to provide legal clarity to the industry.

In August, the U.S. regulator revealed its proposal to introduce two exemptions to the usual securities registration rules. The first exemption would be applicable to qualifying cryptocurrency projects to raise up to $5 million over a four-year period. Meanwhile, the second exemption would allow up to $75 million to be raised each year.

Also, the proposed regulation will introduce a conditional safe harbor for digital assets that have ceased to be investment contracts.

One of the central arguments from Ripple in the 20-page comment letter is related to the difference between the cryptocurrency itself and the investment contract through which it is sold.

According to Alderoty, the proposed regulation follows the rule set by the Supreme Court's historic Howey decision.

The proposed rules suggest that a cryptocurrency does not necessarily have to remain within the scope of securities regulations for life. It all depends on the sale of the cryptocurrency within the investment contract.

It depends on the specific promises made by the issuer. The obligations may no longer apply.

Another important topic that Ripple strongly criticizes is the idea of using the decentralization feature as a core factor in determining whether a cryptocurrency is a security.

#4748. Trump Launches Task Force on Manufactured Homes and Orders Affordability Reforms

President Donald Trump has signed an executive order aimed at housing affordability with days to go before the midterm elections.

The multi-part executive order published Thursday aims to reduce costs for title and mortgage insurance, and promote more affordable manufactured housing. Trump said he wanted to see new technologies that use artificial intelligence, or "super intelligence," to accelerate the housing market.

"The American Dream of homeownership is a vital pillar of our great country," the order states. "It is the policy of my Administration to reduce regulatory barriers to mortgage origination and costs impeding homeownership"

This comes amid a year Americans are concerned about housing affordability. The housing market has been hammered by high interest rates and inflation hitting costs of materials and labor. Mortgage rates are likely to remain near decade highs for the near term.

This year, Congress passed the 21st Century Road to Housing Act after months of sometimes-contentious negotiations. The bill's 45 provisions are aimed at cutting red tape and encouraging the construction of more homes. Trump refused to sign the bill, though it still became law.

Among the ideas in the bill are a pilot program for federal housing agencies to cancel "excessive" mortgage insurance for select borrowers "who appear to satisfy equity and other requirements."

Another provision would establish a task force aimed at promoting innovation in manufactured housing, to be chaired by Federal Housing Finance Agency director Bill Pulte and Secretary of Housing and Urban Development Scott Turner.

Turner in a statement said Trump "is committing to bring even more innovative and affordable housing solutions to market for the American people.”

“For decades, home building has been crippled by regulatory red tape, but the Task Force on Manufactured Housing Innovation can usher in the next generation of housing by modernizing standards and speeding up approvals across government,” added Turner.

Pulte posted to X that the order was "the greatest Housing Executive Order in History" and said it could save "thousands" of dollars for millions of Americans.

The task force would incorporate over government leaders and create recommendations for how to accelerate innovation in the homebuilding industry.

The order directs FHFA to establish a pilot program for Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac aimed at reducing mortgage insurance costs for homeowners.

This program will use automated valuation models to periodically assess home values, and proactively cancel mortgage insurance for borrowers who have met the necessary equity requirements, eliminating the need for excessive documentation and costly appraisals.

Typically, homeowners with at least 20% equity in their home are not required to carry mortgage insurance, which protects the lender in the event of a borrower default.

To lower costs for homeowners who refinance their mortgage, the order also directs the FHFA to expand a program that accepts automated title reviews in place of traditional lender’s title insurance policies.

As well, it seeks to waive appraisals and documentation requirements for certain refinances while ensuring that all cost savings are directly passed on to the borrowers.

In a statement, Shannon McGahn, cheif advocacy officer at the National Association of Realtors, lauded the administration's efforts to promote housing affordability.

"We appreciate the President’s continued focus on this critical issue and look forward to reviewing the Administration’s proposals in greater detail," McGahn said in the statement. "We also look forward to working with federal policymakers to advance practical solutions that lower costs, expand housing opportunities, and help more Americans achieve the dream of homeownership.”

Bob Broeksmit, president and CEO of the Mortgage Bankers Association, said in a statement that the group's lender members "welcome these latest initiatives to reduce the costs of refinancing and expand innovative manufactured housing options for potential homebuyers."

“MBA appreciates the Trump administration’s continued focus on lowering housing costs by reducing unnecessary regulatory burdens, streamlining mortgage processes, and promoting competition across the housing market," said Broeksmit. “MBA will continue to identify and advocate for additional ways to help homebuyers navigate today’s higher interest rate environment and to work with the Administration and Congress to advance policies that deliver meaningful results for homeowners, homebuyers, and renters.”

American Land Title Association CEO Chris Morton said in a statement that efforts to improve housing affordability are "critical. But he warned that any effort to bring title insurance costs down "must also preserve the essential consumer safeguards that protect Americans' most valuable assets."

"Fraud is unaffordable, and the threats facing homeowners are growing," said Morton. "Now more than ever, we need to ensure that efforts to lower housing costs do not shift significant financial risks onto American families or the broader housing finance system."

#4746. Ex-OpenAI science chief Kevin Weil launches Warp Sciences with $215M for drug chemistry

Drug discovery chemistry startup Warp Sciences Inc. launched today with $215 million in new funding, six months after co-founder and Chief Executive Kevin Weil left his job running science initiatives at OpenAI Group PBC.

Warp is building a chemistry contract research organization in the U.S. to supply biotech and pharmaceutical companies with the molecules their drug programs need. Lead optimization analog synthesis is the first service. That work produces variations on a promising molecule for the customer’s scientists to compare as they narrow in on a drug candidate.

Data from those physical experiments will train Warp’s artificial intelligence models, with experienced chemists guiding the work as the lab takes on more automation. Robots will learn lab tasks from chemists wearing cameras and sensor gloves, TechCrunch reported.

Weil said in the launch announcement that the physical sciences have yet to get the lift from AI that has “dramatically accelerated software engineering and mathematics.” Chemistry and biology advance when someone makes a compound and tests it, according to Weil, so AI models for science need “real-world data at scale.”

Sol Reisberg, Warp’s co-founder and chief scientific officer, previously led medicinal chemistry work at Terremoto Biosciences Inc. Overseas chemistry partners he worked with in the past left him worried about protecting the intellectual property behind his programs.

Hiring a boutique U.S. contract research firm instead means paying what he called a “price premium.” Warp aims to match the caliber of those overseas partners from labs in the U.S., at prices that leave the markup out.

A lot of that overseas work ends up in China. Patil was a founding executive at Devoted Health Inc. after serving as the White House’s first chief data scientist. September's $4.80 billion is about 31% above the November 2025 peak, and working backwards from the 72.9% jump puts August near $2.78 billion. Furthermore, on DefiLlama's NEAR Intents page, the trailing 30-day volume shows roughly $4.76 billion and cumulative volume above $33 billion, which fits a month operating at this scale.

For a protocol that only began recording volume in December 2024, that is a remarkably steep climb.

In real estate, technology is often sold as the solution to an agent’s productivity problems. Frank Vazquez believes the better approach is to start with the problems themselves.

Vazquez — broker-owner of Scottsdale, Arizona-based Incyte Realty — is building an in-house technology ecosystem designed to automate repetitive tasks, improve consumer service and help agents focus on growing their businesses.

Drawing on his background in information systems architecture and software development, he has developed proprietary tools for customer relationship management (CRM), marketing, home search, recruiting and agent coaching.

For other brokerage owners, the lesson is to identify a measurable problem before committing resources to a solution. A tool should make a process easier, improve service or help agents produce better results — not simply add another application to learn, Vazquez said.

One of Incyte’s most ambitious projects is Morgan, an AI assistant that handles front-desk reception, property sign calls, text conversations and appointment scheduling.

The system also supports recruiting, agent onboarding, market-pattern analysis and the generation of materials agents can request from their phones.

Vazquez said he’s developing more advanced capabilities intended to let the system learn from experience and improve its operations.

For other brokerage owners, Vazquez’s experience points to a practical sequence. Start with consumer needs, ask agents where they struggle, build tools around specific problems, introduce them into everyday workflows and measure the results.

The competitive advantage, ultimately, is not AI alone. It is the ability to use technology to help people do their jobs better, Vazquez said.

It's best to separate the two numbers, because they do different jobs. Staked JUP is locked by its owners, and the owners can start unlocking it at any time. Bought-back JUP is taken from the market by the protocol, and it isn't returned to the open market by a holder's decision.

Still, a token with a third of its circulating supply locked, and more than a third removed from the market once buybacks are counted, has a very different float from one that is fully liquid.

A new software tool from Amazon aims to make switching from Nvidia to the company’s own AI chips about as simple as changing a setting.

Until now, developers who wanted to run PyTorch code on Trainium had to modify it to call programming interfaces specific to Amazon’s chips, said Colin Brace, vice president of technology software at Annapurna Labs, Amazon’s chip-design unit. With TorchNeuron, the same code runs after a basic configuration change.

“If you know PyTorch, then you know Trainium,” Brace said in an interview.

Nvidia dominates the market for AI chips, partly because much of the industry’s AI software was built around its CUDA platform. Adapting that code to other hardware can take months of engineering work. That has made many companies reluctant to switch, even as Amazon, Google and others have developed their own chips.

TorchNeuron is part of a broader push by tech giants to loosen Nvidia’s grip on AI computing, even as they remain among its biggest customers.

Asked about Amazon’s relationship with Nvidia, Brace said the company has “always been about choice,” pointing to another high-profile example: AWS offers Intel processors alongside its own Graviton chips.

Amazon plans to release TorchNeuron as open-source software on Tuesday in conjunction with the PyTorch Conference in San Jose, Calif.

Trainium’s biggest users so far are AI labs with close financial ties to Amazon. Anthropic, in which Amazon has invested billions, uses the chips to train and run its Claude models. OpenAI agreed in February to use 2 gigawatts of Trainium capacity as part of a deal in which Amazon is investing $50 billion in the company.

Brace said Amazon’s goal has always been wide use of the chips, calling TorchNeuron “an accelerant” along those lines.

Amazon’s chip business traces back to its 2015 acquisition of Annapurna Labs, an Israeli chipmaker, for a reported $350 million. The Annapurna team went on to design Trainium as well as Amazon’s Graviton processors.

In his annual shareholder letter in April, Amazon CEO Andy Jassy wrote that the company’s chip business, including Trainium and Graviton, had an annual revenue run rate of more than $20 billion. To make sure TorchNeuron works with the huge variety of AI models that developers build, Amazon tests it every night against more than 100,000 models from Hugging Face, the popular online hub for open-source AI, Brace said.

Brace said the timing of the project reflects changes in PyTorch itself. The framework has matured, and its maintainers have been working to make it less dependent on any one kind of hardware.

Amazon has contributed to that effort, removing code that tied PyTorch to specific chips. Newer versions of PyTorch can detect which hardware they’re running on, he said, so in some cases developers don’t need to change their code at all.

And now, the once idyllic listing opens with a very different sales pitch: “Property has been RED TAGGED and is LAND VALUE.”

It may be an extreme example of a problem California now expects to confront on a much larger scale.

Without intervention, 24% to 75% of the state’s beaches could disappear seasonally or permanently by 2100 as seas rise, according to models cited in California’s newly released Fifth Climate Change Assessment. It warns that “highly developed areas of Southern California [face] especially severe losses.”

A beach is sediment in motion: Waves and currents pick up and deposit sand, continually erasing and redrawing the shoreline.

Andy Carter, the listing agent for Seaside Walk, has worked in Southern California real estate for more than 20 years and is a third-generation Long Beach resident. He says erosion had worsened gradually for years—but what has happened this fall is different.

“The entire front of the house is basically slowly cracking off and falling into the Pacific,” Carter tells Realtor.com®.

Damage began with Hurricane Marie in September, worsened with Hurricane Polo, and continued in October as Tropical Storm Rachel sent another southerly swell toward the coast. Each round of surf carried away more sand, narrowing the beach that normally absorbs some of the waves’ energy before it reaches the homes behind it.

It's the heartbreaking example of a process that is playing out en masse in coastal markets across the country.

“Erosion can often be seen as a terrible thing for buyers and homeowners, but it's a critical part of maintaining a healthy coastal ecosystem,” Bryan McCormack, a coastal processes specialist with WHOI Sea Grant and Cape Cod Cooperative Extension, told Realtor.com in July.

U.S. Geological Survey exposure data shows how much existing property intersects modeled shoreline-change hazards—the areas where the shoreline is projected to move inland as rising seas and erosion reshape the coast.

At 1 meter, or about 3.3 feet, of sea-level rise, roughly $6.6 billion in combined land and improvement parcel value falls within those zones. At 3 meters, or about 9.8 feet, the figure grows to nearly $24.7 billion.

But those dollar figures use a 2020 reference year and parcel values—not current market prices. Since then, values have skyrocketed.

“Home sale prices have risen about 39% on average in these counties since 2020, so the actual value of exposed property is likely higher than the 2020-based assessments,” explains Hannah Jones, senior economist at Realtor.com.

With less sand arriving naturally, the city spends roughly $1 million to $1.5 million a year moving it back.

They're making a different calculation than the typical buyer: not that the hazard will disappear, but that oceanfront land may remain valuable enough to justify the risk—especially if the beach in front of it continues to be defended.

“Once they finally build out the beach,” Carter says, “those home values are going to go up significantly.”

And the more successfully the shoreline is protected, the longer that value—and the pressure to keep defending it—can persist.

#4721. Why Paying Launchpad Users in USDC Instead of a Native Token Is a Smart (and Risky) Choice

AI-assisted summary, reviewed by NullTX editors.

When a launchpad on Circle's new Arc chain reports paying users more than $5.5 million in plain USDC, it commands attention.

Argus, which went live alongside Arc's mainnet in September, is reporting more than 184,000 token launches and over $455 million in swap volume. However, the bigger story lies in the asset chosen to distribute to users.

The figures originate from Argus's own milestone post and should be evaluated as project-reported data. Meanwhile, one caution on the count is that the headline noted 180,000 launches, while the bullet list listed more than 184,000. Naturally, a counter moving this fast will disagree with itself, making the higher, more recent figure the logical baseline.

Some quick math offers useful context. Specifically, $455 million across 184,000 launches averages roughly $2,470 per launch. In reality, that average is almost certainly driven by a handful of high-volume tokens carrying thousands of inactive ones. Furthermore, the $5.5 million paid out equals roughly 1.2% of swap volume, proving that most of the capital flowing through Argus represents pure trading activity rather than payouts.

As a result, that ratio becomes critical when evaluating the platform's long-term structure.

Argus is a permissionless launchpad on Arc, Circle's EVM-compatible chain, which went live on September 16, 2026. According to the Argus documentation, each token opens a Uniswap v4 pool with a custom tax hook in the same transaction as the launch. Therefore, the full supply sits in one liquidity position, allowing trading to start immediately without a bonding curve or a later migration step. For readers looking to analyze why this architecture functions, Uniswap's v4 documentation details the mechanics of hooks.

Initially, creators set buy and sell taxes between 0% and 10% at deployment. Swap fees and taxes then flow through a splitter. Per the project's description, 10% goes to the Argus treasury while the remaining share follows the creator's parameters: a creator allocation, buybacks and burns, added liquidity, or USDC rewards for holders. Additionally, a brief anti-snipe tax applies immediately after launch.

This mechanism stands out as the most critical detail for market participants. Crucially, the USDC distribution is not drawn from a marketing budget or treasury reserve. As far as the project's design shows, it is skimmed directly from trading taxes paid by traders swapping each token. Because holder rewards are configured on a per-token basis, a creator can choose to route a slice of fees to holders in USDC, meaning these configurations vary significantly and are not guaranteed across all launches.

Ultimately, that structure aligns the payout far closer to revenue sharing than a traditional speculative airdrop. That transparency eliminates the need to speculate on a future token price for value to exist. Nevertheless, the reality remains plain: without sustained volume, payouts drop to zero.

Overall, four core advantages back the decision to pay rewards in USDC.

However, evaluating the model requires looking beyond the potential upside.

First, a USDC reward is only as durable as the trading activity behind it. If launch volume cools, the payout shrinks in tandem. Conversely, governance tokens at least carry speculative upside potential, which stable rewards cannot offer.

Second, there is no governance or equity stake involved. A native token offers users voting power and a stake in the protocol's future, whereas a dollar payout yields cash rewards and nothing further.

Third, creators define the parameters per token, meaning 184,000 launches yield 184,000 distinct configurations. Consequently, a reward structure on one token reveals nothing about the terms of another.

Fourth, regulatory scrutiny remains a factor for any mechanism resembling profit sharing. After all, the Argus fee design includes an explicit "dividend" slot. Teams and participants should consult legal expertise before relying heavily on this structure.

Moving forward, three key factors will determine the model's viability. The primary indicator is the ongoing ratio of payouts to volume, which sits near 1.2% today. Another critical metric is the percentage of tokens that actually generate payouts for holders, as broad averages hide heavy concentration. Finally, the platform's trajectory depends on whether Argus eventually introduces its own governance layer, especially given that the ARGUS token already trades in secondary markets.

In conclusion, Argus presents a practical case study for a model the industry has long resisted: rewarding users without minting a fresh speculative token. Admittedly, it relies on trading fee extraction rather than novel mechanics. Even so, yield that survives market volatility without diluting token holders remains a rare feature in digital asset launches.

Developers access cloud-based large language models through application programming interfaces. Each LLM provider uses a different API, which means that software written for one provider’s API can’t be easily ported to another. Switching providers often requires developers to make extensive code changes.

Open Responses, the first of the four projects donated to the AAIF, addresses the challenge. It provides a standardized template for implementing AI model APIs. Developers can move code between APIs that use Open Responses without making major changes.

The technology was donated by OpenAI, which modeled it after the API through which it provides access to its LLMs. That means OpenAI-powered workloads can be easily moved to APIs based on Open Responses and vice versa. Plus, the fact that many developers are already familiar with the company’s APIs should ease adoption.

The second contribution that AAIF announced today came from Anthropic. The company donated Agent Skills, an open-source project it released last December. It provides a standardized format for implementing skills, which are collections of resources that improve an AI agent’s output quality. Those resources can include instructions that explain how to carry out a task, illustrative examples and automation scripts.

Packaging skills in a standardized format makes it easier to share them between development teams. There’s no need to change how the files are structured before connecting them to a workload. Organizing data consistently also makes it easier to process for AI agents, which lowers the risk of hallucinations.

AI labs train their models in sandboxes, technology environments that simulate the infrastructure on which an LLM will be expected to run in production. For example, a company building a vibe coding model might create sandboxes that simulate developer workstations. Sandboxes are the focus of OpenEnv and OpenSandbox, the two other projects that the AAIF announced today. They were donated by Hugging Face and Alibaba, respectively.

The sandboxes used in an AI project are coordinated by a training system that automates repetitive management tasks. OpenEnv reduces the amount of work involved in switching training systems. OpenSandbox, in turn, provides a set of APIs for managing sandboxes. It eases tasks such as connecting agents that run inside sandboxes to external applications.

#4718. Corporate brand vs. loan officer brand: Mortgage companies need both

Mortgage companies perform best when corporate branding and loan officer branding are designed to work together. Set non-negotiables for identity and compliance, then give loan officers room to show expertise, specialties and local market connection.  AI Summary

A borrower finds a loan officer on social media. They like the loan officer’s communication style, appreciate the educational content they share and trust their guidance through the mortgage process. Then they click over to the lender’s website and encounter a completely different experience. The personality and connection that initially caught their attention get lost in the larger corporate brand.

The opposite can happen, too. A loan officer builds a strong individual presence with their own messaging, content and local reputation, but the connection to the mortgage company they represent becomes almost invisible.

I have seen this tension firsthand working in both corporate and branch-level mortgage marketing. At one company with a strong corporate brand, new loan officers sometimes came in with personal brands they had spent years building and had little interest in adopting the company’s identity. I also saw loan officers consider competing opportunities due to branding and marketing strength.

Those experiences changed how I look at the relationship between the two. Mortgage companies should not have to choose between protecting a recognizable corporate brand and giving loan officers room to establish their own presence. A better strategy is to determine where consistency matters and where individuality strengthens the overall brand.

There is a difference between providing consistency and making every loan officer look identical.

If hundreds of loan officers post the same graphic with the same caption directing borrowers to nearly identical profile pages, the company maintains brand consistency but does little to help its loan officers compete in their individual markets.

Relationships are a big part of how loan officers grow their business, but the relationships look different from one loan officer to the next. Some are known for working with first-time homebuyers, while others have a strong referral network with real estate professionals in their area. That experience and local connection are often what make a borrower or referral partner choose one loan officer over another.

Their marketing should reflect that. Loan officers need room to show potential borrowers who they are, what they know and how they can help, while still representing the mortgage company and its brand consistently.

The other extreme creates its own problems. When loan officers develop their own marketing without enough connection to the corporate brand, borrowers can encounter completely different versions of the same company depending on which loan officer they find first.

You might see it in something as simple as an old logo, a graphic that looks nothing like the company’s marketing or a social media page that leaves you wondering which lender the loan officer actually works for. It can become a much bigger issue when the information being shared about loans or programs isn’t accurate or raises compliance concerns.

Corporate marketing can create the balance by establishing what needs to stay consistent while giving loan officers room to make their marketing their own, share their expertise and connect with the communities they serve.

Mortgage companies can maintain control over the parts of the brand that require consistency without controlling every part of how a loan officer presents themselves.

A social media template, for example, can lock in branding and required compliance language while leaving room for a loan officer to add local information, their own perspective or messaging relevant to their audience. Loan officer pages can follow a consistent company structure while leaving space for individual bios, specialties, reviews and local market information.

Marketing teams should also stay close to the people working in their markets. In my brand marketing experience, corporate materials were sometimes too broad for a specific market, or there simply weren’t enough resources for what the loan officers needed locally. At the same time, materials created for individual loan officers didn’t always align with what corporate marketing wanted to present. That disconnect left branch teams trying to meet local marketing needs while still operating within corporate expectations.

Regular input from loan offices and branch teams can help corporate marketing understand what is actually needed in the field and create resources that work at both levels.

Education is another important part of that framework. Providing a library of approved content solves an immediate need. Helping loan officers build a professional digital presence, communicate their expertise and create content within established brand and compliance guidelines helps them use those resources more effectively.

Corporate and loan officer branding work best when a clear strategy explains how the two work together. Mortgage companies should define what needs to remain consistent, including brand identity, compliance requirements, core messaging and the overall borrower experience, while giving loan officers room to build their own presence through their expertise, personality and connection to the markets they serve.

The goal is to protect the company’s brand while giving loan officers enough room to build something recognizable within it.

Mortgage companies don’t need to choose between building their corporate brand and building the brands of their loan officers. When the strategy is designed correctly, the success of one should increase the value of the other.

#4714. Burn Announcements vs. Burn Proof: How to Verify a Token Burn On-Chain

AI-assisted summary, reviewed by NullTX editors.

The cheapest word in crypto is "burned." After all, it costs nothing to type, it looks great in a headline, and it usually moves a price chart for an afternoon.

Proof, however, is entirely different. It sits quietly on a public ledger waiting for anyone to inspect it, yet almost nobody takes the time to look. So, when Lighter announced it had burned 2,452,017 LIT, representing the tokens it bought back through the end of Q3 2026, I looked it up.

To start, Lighter's official announcement states that it executed a burn of 2,452,017 LIT, which matches the amount programmatically repurchased through Q3 2026. Conveniently, the announcement linked directly to an Etherscan transaction and that is the receipt truly worth reading.

When you open the page, here is what immediately stands out. First, the transaction succeeded, landing in block 26,156,993 on October 9, 2026, at 7:37 PM UTC. Furthermore, Etherscan lists the transferred asset as 2,452,017 LIT and identifies the recipient as 0x000000000000000000000000000000000000dEaD, which is labeled as a null address. Consequently, this matches the team's public statement to the exact figure. At the time of writing, Etherscan estimated the burned tokens' total value at roughly $9.5 million, though that figure naturally fluctuates alongside market price.

Always begin with the status field. If a transaction fails, it displays an error and transfers nothing, regardless of how convincing the press release sounded. Next, verify the block number and confirmations. While a freshly broadcast transaction can still be dropped or replaced, a transaction with thousands of confirmations is about as settled as Ethereum gets. In this case, it already had over 18,000 confirmations when I reviewed it.

This step represents the classic trap that many observers fall into. On this transaction, the top-level "To" field points to an address labeled "Lighter: Treasury," which is actually a Safe smart account (a multi-signature wallet) rather than the burn address itself.

However, this structure is completely normal. In practice, an authorized party at Lighter signed an interaction that instructed the Treasury Safe to transfer the tokens out. As a result, the primary "To" field simply displays the wallet initiating the movement. To find the actual destination, you must scroll down to the token transfer section. This area reveals who sent what to whom. Looking there, we can clearly see that the Treasury transferred 2,452,017 LIT directly to the dead address.

Unfortunately, bad actors rely on people skipping this crucial check. Because anyone can deploy a fraudulent token called "LIT" with identical icons and tickers, verifying the contract address is vital. On Etherscan, the transfer references contract 0x232ce3bd40fcd6f80f3d55a522d03f25df784ee2. Therefore, before trusting any burn report, always ensure this contract address matches the official one published by the project. Since the address wasn't immediately listed on the main Lighter docs landing page, you should verify it against their primary documentation index or official community channels before relying on it for reporting. Ultimately, burning a fake token proves nothing.

The destination address, 0x…dEaD, serves as the most widely recognized burn destination in crypto. Nevertheless, using it is a community convention rather than an unchangeable law of mathematics. Because no known party holds its private key and the statistical likelihood of generating it is effectively zero, tokens sent here are considered permanently removed from circulation. You can easily view the address's ongoing balances on Etherscan's dedicated page, while the token's specific holder list reflects how much LIT remains trapped there.

Here is an important nuance that standard guides often miss: while transferring tokens to a dead address removes them from active circulation, it does not automatically reduce the contract's official total supply figure. That metric only decreases if a built-in burn function is explicitly executed on the contract itself. Thus, whenever a tracking platform reports a supply reduction, check whether it simply subtracted the dead address balance or pulled a lower overall total supply. Because I haven't audited LIT's internal contract mechanics, you should check the supply figures on the token explorer page directly.

While a transaction proves that tokens were destroyed, it doesn't automatically prove they originated from the exact source claimed. To close that loop, you need to cross-reference the burn data with recorded buybacks. For instance, DefiLlama's LIT page lists Q3 2026 buybacks totaling $7.42 million, following $14.59 million in Q1 and $7.07 million in Q2. Dividing that $7.42 million by 2,452,017 LIT yields an estimated average price of around $3.03 per token. Although this is an informal back-of-the-envelope calculation rather than an official accounting metric, it aligns well with LIT's prevailing market price during that quarter.

In addition, Lighter previously noted that it might burn unallocated treasury LIT instead of the exact units repurchased on the market, citing that both methods remain economically equivalent for holders. Furthermore, as summarized in a June 30 tokenomics update, the team stated that burns would route tokens from the exchange to an Ethereum burn address, a process this transaction follows precisely. Even so, prudent analysts should continue checking the Safe's transaction history to confirm that incoming buyback receipts align with outgoing burn amounts over time.

Looking at the broader picture, 2.45 million tokens represent roughly 1% of the 250 million LIT currently in circulation, based on DefiLlama data at the time of review. While that is a meaningful reduction, it remains a relatively modest portion of the overall one-billion maximum supply. More importantly, the broader trajectory reveals a key insight: buybacks shifted from $14.59 million in Q1 to $7.07 million in Q2 and $7.42 million in Q3. This trend indicates that burn size is tied directly to ongoing protocol revenue rather than a fixed calendar schedule.

Ultimately, what stands out most about this audit is how little time it required. Within just a few minutes on a block explorer, an unverified announcement was transformed into a verified public record. Lighter deserves credit for including the transaction link directly in their announcement, though many projects still fail to do so. The next time a token burn headline crosses your feed, look for that transaction link first. If it isn't provided, that absence alone tells you plenty.

A central mechanics puzzle faces anyone evaluating a staked crypto ETF: the fund promises daily liquidity to shareholders on open market days, yet it plans to lock up at least 90% of its underlying assets in a staking contract that requires 21 days to unbond.

The headline target of 90% is far less rigid in practice than it appears on paper. According to the prospectus, the sponsor expects to stake at least 90% of the trust's total INJ holdings under normal market conditions.

The remaining balance is withheld at the sponsor's sole discretion to facilitate anticipated redemptions, settle fund expenses, or safeguard trust operations. Consequently, the 90% target represents an operational goal rather than a strict cap, leaving an unstaked liquidity buffer that effectively functions as a liquid checking account alongside a locked savings reserve.

The core operational tension stems from Injective's protocol rules, which enforce a mandatory 21-day unbonding period. During this window, unbonded INJ tokens yield no staking rewards and remain completely non-transferable. While the filing explicitly notes that redelegating assets between active network validators is instantaneous, withdrawing underlying coins from the network remains a slow process. Therefore, managing daily share redemptions requires a structured liquidity strategy.

Crucially, standard secondary market trading does not impact the fund's underlying cryptocurrency holdings. When an investor sells ETF shares on a public exchange, the transaction takes place directly with another market participant, leaving the trust's INJ balance entirely untouched. The trust itself interacts exclusively with Authorized Participants (APs), large financial institutions that process share creations and redemptions in block units, specifically set at 10,000-share baskets.

As a result, the fund's cash reserves and unstaked INJ are only drawn upon when an Authorized Participant formally redeems a basket. On typical trading days, AP redemptions represent only a fraction of total trading volume.

The 90% staking model works effectively because the vast majority of daily liquidity is absorbed internally by secondary market participants trading among themselves.

When an Authorized Participant initiates a redemption, the sponsor relies on multiple liquidity layers.

According to the filing, the trust may sell or transfer INJ as needed to generate cash proceeds for redemptions or fund fees, potentially utilizing third-party digital asset brokers to execute trades. In addition, block-level staking rewards accrue continuously to the net asset value (NAV). While these rewards are generally restaked, portions can be liquidated to cover immediate liquidity demands or expenses.

The fund also maintains a formal Liquidity Risk Management Policy that evaluates unbonding timelines, staking parameters, external credit sources, and redemption settlement windows. While the prospectus notes that the trust may secure credit facilities as a liquidity backstop, no formal credit agreements were finalized as of the filing date. Consequently, key elements of the fund's ultimate safety net are defined structurally but remain subject to implementation.

Despite the detailed framework, several operational questions remain unaddressed in current amendments.

Specifically, the filing does not detail explicit procedures for scenarios where redemption demands exceed available unstaked INJ buffers, nor does it specify exact terms for temporary redemption suspensions. Furthermore, a minor structural ambiguity exists within the paperwork: while one section states that INJ is distributed or sold during redemptions, the plan of distribution notes that Authorized Participants will not directly handle underlying INJ. Subsequent SEC amendments are expected to clarify these mechanics before final approval.

In practice, fund managers typically utilize rolling unbonding schedules, initiating staggered token withdrawals in advance of expected redemption cycles to maintain a steady stream of incoming liquidity. While this represents standard institutional portfolio management rather than explicit filing text, it illustrates how liquidity managers bridge protocol delays.

Earning staking yields within an ETF structure introduces distinct trade-offs, which are highlighted throughout the filing's risk disclosures. The prospectus noted an estimated annualized staking reward of roughly 6.8% as of mid-September 2026, but cautioned that yields could compress significantly as total network bonding approaches its 60% target.

Additionally, total net yield is reduced by staking fees split among the provider, sponsor, and custodian, percentages that remain unquantified in current drafts. While slashing penalties were zero at the time of filing, network governance updates can adjust these parameters automatically. Furthermore, staked assets carry no FDIC insurance protections.

From a tax perspective, staking yield distributions may be classified as taxable ordinary income for shareholders, though definitive guidance remains pending.

Ultimately, a staked crypto ETF functions by leveraging two distinct tiers of market liquidity: peer-to-peer exchange trading among secondary investors and direct trust liquidity for primary redemptions.

The 90% staking rule relies primarily on secondary market volume to absorb daily turnover, backed by an unstaked buffer, ongoing yield accruals, and discretionary credit lines for primary redemptions.

While this framework operates smoothly under normal market conditions, its ultimate stress test will depend on how effectively the fund manages concentrated, large-scale redemption shocks.

AI has already carved out a massive role in how businesses interact with customers. Now it’s showing up on the other side of the conversation. First, there’s the question of identity. Most businesses have no reliable way to know whether they are talking to a customer, a customer’s authorized agent or something pretending to be one. That infrastructure needs to be in place before anything else matters.

Second, you need visibility into what is already happening. You need to be able to see how often an agent is showing up in a support queue, what it’s asking for and how often it’s succeeding.

Then there’s the issue of the policies themselves. A traditional review to ensure accuracy is no longer sufficient. Businesses need to audit their policies, line by line, and stress-test every potential ambiguity that could turn into a vulnerability. Find the gaps before an agent does.

Resist the instinct to default to blocking. Throughout history, we’ve seen what happens to companies that try to close a door that’s going to be kicked down regardless. Search engines that tried to wall off the open web lost to the ones that stayed open, and the same pattern is likely to repeat here. Open does not mean undefended. That would be a mistake. But the shift is happening, and CX organizations can’t be lulled into a reactive posture.

There’s a lot of noise right now about what these personal AI agents should and will be allowed to do. The dust has clearly not settled, and some very important questions hang in the air. But make no mistake: This will not be a passing trend or a footnote in the broader story of AI innovation. This is a massive shift in how businesses interact with customers, and the companies that start getting ready for it now are the ones who will be prepared when it arrives at their front door.
