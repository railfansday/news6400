#4850. Amazon’s new AI devices can listen to conversations and suggest things to buy — if you opt in

by Todd Bishop on Oct 15, 2026 at 5:39 amOctober 15, 2026 at 5:39 am

Bee, the AI wearables startup Amazon acquired last year, makes devices that remember what people say in everyday conversations. A new update, announced Thursday morning, includes a feature that turns those passing remarks into shopping suggestions on Amazon.com.

For example, as described by Amazon, someone might mention offhand that they’re out of dish soap, and a product recommendation would show up on Amazon.com and in the Bee mobile and desktop apps. Amazon pitches it as a way for people to remember to buy things they need.

It’s the kind of thing many people have long suspected their phones of doing secretly, listening to conversations and serving up related content or ads. Researchers haven’t found evidence of that. Either way, Amazon’s twist is to do it openly — and to make it voluntary.

The feature is opt-in, meaning it won’t happen unless users choose to connect their Bee accounts to Amazon.com, and Bee says it won’t share data with Amazon without user consent.

The Amazon.com integration requires Bee Pro, a $12 monthly subscription that also includes unlimited conversation summaries and the ability to ask Alexa+ about things Bee has captured.

New hardware: The feature is part of a broader announcement that includes the first new Bee devices under Amazon’s ownership: Bee Frontier, a $74.99 wristband that can also clip onto clothing, and Bee Eclipse, which attaches magnetically to the back of a phone and starts at $99.99.

Bee Frontier, available now, has dual microphones, up to seven days of battery life and IP67 water resistance. Bee Eclipse, about the size of a credit card, runs more than 21 days on a charge and will be available soon, Amazon says.

Amazon says Bee lets people pay attention to a conversation instead of taking notes, keeping track of details such as when and where they agreed to meet a friend. The devices turn conversations into summaries, to-dos and reminders.

Users can read their summaries in the Bee mobile app or a new desktop app, formatted for different kinds of conversations, such as lectures, care plans and brainstorming sessions. Bee also picks up on recurring themes in conversations and suggests goals and actions.

Bee connects to apps including Gmail, Google Calendar and Microsoft Outlook. After a call or meeting, it can list action items, draft follow-up emails or create calendar invites based on the conversation.

Users can also link Bee to Apple HealthKit and to their photos. Most of those features, including the desktop app and third-party integrations, also require Bee Pro.

Without a subscription, Bee users get conversation summaries and 14 days of memory. New customers get 30 days of Bee Pro free.

How Bee handles privacy: Amazon says Bee runs on a system it calls Bee Private Compute, which processes conversations in an isolated environment where “no one, including Amazon, can access your data.” Audio is deleted after it’s processed, and transcripts are encrypted with keys controlled by the user’s device.

Both new devices turn on and off with a single button and show a light when they’re running. For phone calls, Bee Eclipse prompts users to get permission from the other people on the call each time it starts, according to Amazon.

Bee and Amazon: Amazon acquired Bee in July 2025 for an undisclosed price. At the time, the San Francisco startup, led by co-founders Maria de Lourdes Zollo and Ethan Sutin, had raised $7 million and sold a $49.99 wristband called Bee Pioneer.

Thursday’s update connects Bee to two of Amazon’s biggest consumer businesses: its online store, through the product recommendations; and Alexa+, the new version of its longtime voice assistant, by letting users ask Alexa+ on an Echo device about things Bee has captured.

Amazon isn’t the only tech giant betting on AI devices.

Bee also competes with startups such as Plaud, Omi and Friend, which make AI recorders and pendants that listen to conversations, and with AI notetaking apps such as Granola and Otter that work on a phone, with no extra device required.

On another front, Amazon is fighting to keep other companies’ AI agents out of its store. Last month it blocked Meta’s Muse assistant from shopping on Amazon.com, after earlier moves against shopping agents from Perplexity, Google and OpenAI.

#4846. Code testing startup Blacksmith becomes a unicorn after raising $66M in Series C funding

Blacksmith Software Inc., a continuous integration cloud startup that helps companies to test and verify new code before they ship it into production, says it’s now valued at $1 billion after closing on a $66 million Series C round of funding today.

The round was led by Sequoia Capital and saw participation from Peak XV Partners, Y Combinator and GV, and brings Blacksmith’s total amount raised so far to $125 million. It comes just two months after it raised $45 million in a Series B round led by Peak XV. Prior to that, it raised $10 million in September 2025.

Blacksmith came into being in 2024. It’s led by its co-founder and Chief Executive Aditya Jayaprakash and it’s offering a fairly novel service in the shape of a “CI cloud,” with the CI standing for “continuous integration. That’s the process of automating builds and testing software before it’s merged into an existing codebase and deployed in production.

Before Blacksmith was a thing, most companies would handle CI themselves, running those jobs in the cloud on servers hosted by Amazon Web Services Inc., Microsoft Azure, Google Cloud or someone else. But nowadays, they can go with Blacksmith instead, which has bought its own servers and built a dedicated CI cloud that spans multiple global locations.

The startup writes its own software to run those CI jobs. One of its main advantages is that it can automate much of the once-manual set up work required before each new piece of code can be tested.

In a blog post announcing today’s round, Jayaprakash explained that before the advent of artificial intelligence coding assistants, most developers used to spend approximately half their time writing code and the other half waiting for it to build and pass various tests. But the emergence of AI coding tools like Cursor, Anthropic PBC’s Claude and OpenAI Group PBC’s Codex, the process of creating new code has been made “extremely easy,” he said.

“Now the bottleneck has shifted to the second piece, which is: how do I actually validate all this code?” Jayaprakash told Fortune in an interview.

This phenomenon is best evidenced by Anthropic, which said last month that its code validation workload has grown more than 25-fold in the last six months as a result of Claude taking over most of the coding duties from its human developers. Blacksmith hasn’t quite managed to fix this bottleneck, but its CI cloud does ease the pressure somewhat. “Companies bring us their CI workloads and we run them faster than they could anywhere else, with zero operational overhead,” Jayaprakash explained.

Part of the problem with CI work is its unpredictable nature, with demand jumping from zero to tens of thousands of central processing units and then dropping to nothing again when the testing gets done and there’s nothing waiting in the pipeline. Jayaprakash said his company has focused on building its cloud infrastructure in a way that absorbs this unpredictability.

Because the company owns its own hardware rather than renting it, it has more control over its performance, enabling it to run through customer’s CI workloads faster. However, Jayaprakash admitted that this has caused a problem for the startup, as it’s now exposed to the global shortage of components such as memory.

Capacity planning has become one of the most challenging aspects of running the company, he said, as demand often jumps whenever a new frontier model is launched. For instance, when Anthropic debuted Claude Fable 5 in July, its CI workload volume shot up more than 40% in a single day.

Though Blacksmith is helping companies to deal with the deluge of AI-generated code, there is a risk that AI model vendors could become rivals, assuming code validation duties themselves.

That said, there can be no denying Blacksmith’s traction so far. The startup says more than 10,000 companies now use its platform for their CI work, up from around 5,000 in August. Some of its best known customers include LangChain, Supabase and Granola.

In early 2026, it was running about 5 million code checks per week, and that number has now grown to about 90 million, with annual recurring revenue up 17 times over the same period. Jayaprakash said the company’s revenue is now in the “tens of millions of dollars,” and some of its largest clients spend more than $1 million annually.

To cater to its growing customer base, Blacksmith said it will use most of the money from today’s round on buying more servers to build out its global CI cloud. It also wants to expand its team of about 40 employees to 75 by the end of the year.

The dormant wallets of one of this year's largest crypto heists are moving again. Blockchain trackers Onchain Lens and Lookonchain have recorded a mass withdrawal of Ethereum stolen this spring from the decentralized platform Drift Protocol, based on Arkham data.

After two months of complete silence, the attackers activated the addresses under their control and began methodically transferring assets to the Tornado Cash mixer. The current transactions are the second major laundering attempt: in July, the hackers withdrew about $44 million through the mixer in a similar manner. #4844. The Biggest Credit Hurdle for Renters Who Want To Buy

What’s the biggest payment you make every month? If you’re a renter, it’s probably your rent.

But did you know that you can pay your rent on time, every time, for years, and it may never show up in your credit history? It’s pretty frustrating, especially if you’d like to buy a home someday and you’re trying to build your credit.

The good thing is that’s changing right now. Here’s what you need to know and why it could open a door you thought was closed.

You’re not the only one frustrated. According to Zillow, nearly a third of applicants (32.88%) get denied a mortgage due to insufficient credit history – not missed payments or too much debt. Just not enough credit history for a lender to say “yes.”

It’s the most common hurdle would-be buyers have. As Zillow explains:

“Nearly one-third of mortgage applicants are denied a loan because of insufficient credit history, making it the most common reason for a denial.”

But there’s finally a way in now that how credit gets measured is changing.

As Michael Sherman, General Manager and Senior VP at Zillow Rentals, explains:

“Affordability is the headline in housing right now, and access to financing is a very important part of the story. Credit history is a barrier standing in the way of too many potential buyers. Rent reporting is a direct way to address that.”

Here’s what Sherman is talking about. New credit models are emerging that take on-time rent and some utility payments into consideration to help renters get credit for the bills they already pay.

VantageScore is one company with a new credit model that has been approved for use by the Federal Housing Finance Agency (FHFA). And that roll out could have a big impact on renters like you.

A recent study by VantageScore found that nearly 4 million U.S. renters could finally become eligible for a mortgage if their on-time rent payments were counted as part of their credit history. Now, this isn’t a guarantee that it will suddenly count for every would-be buyer, but it does crack open the door.

Here’s the catch though, you still have to walk through it.

There are more options available to build credit, but you have to be proactive. Your rent won’t necessarily count unless you make sure it does. And the sooner you take credit for your payments, the better.

Here’s how to get started based on a few tips from Experian, the Consumer Financial Protection Bureau (CFPB), and FICO:

Ask your landlord or property manager if your rent is being reported. If it’s not, opt in.

If your landlord doesn’t offer rent reporting, there are services available. Be sure to check fees and which bureaus they provide your data to.

Check your credit reports for errors. Mistakes happen and fixing them is one of the quickest ways to strengthen your profile. The CFPB offers step-by-step instructions on how to fix errors on your report.

Follow basic, important credit rules. Pay your bills on time, keep your card balances low, and don’t apply for a bunch of new credit at once.

Don’t close any accounts – even if you have paid them off and don’t use them often.

Diversify your profile by getting a secured credit card or becoming an authorized user on someone else’s account to help you build history beyond rent.

These steps take a little time and effort, but it could be worth it if it starts building up your credit history.

Just remember, not every lender uses the same scoring model and there are more changes on the horizon. So, the sooner you get credit for the work you do, the better.

Even if you’re not sure when you might be ready to buy a home, talking to a lender is a good place to start. A professional loan officer will help you make a plan and help you figure out a timeline to get ready to officially apply and buy.

Space propulsion company Agile Space Industries is expanding its footprint to the Pacific Northwest, signing a sublease for a new engineering office in Bellevue, Wash.

Situated in the Sunset North office complex along Interstate 90, the nearly 5,740-square-foot space will initially house a half-dozen existing employees while scaling up to accommodate an engineering team of 20 to 30 people.

The effort is being led by Chief Technology Officer Jake Mills, a former propulsion engineer and crew member at Jeff Bezos’ Kent-based Blue Origin space venture. Mills joined Durango, Colo.-based Agile in June and the Bellevue hub will try to tap into more of the Seattle region’s deep aerospace talent pool for systems, design, and propulsion engineering.

Started in 2009 as Advanced Mobile Propulsion Technologies, Agile specializes in in-space chemical propulsion systems for satellites, lunar landers, and national security missions. Unlike booster rockets that launch payloads off the planet, the company’s hardware — including thrusters, propellant tanks, and integrated propulsion systems — takes over once spacecraft reach orbit, navigating everything from commercial constellations to lunar exploration.

“The talent pool there, manufacturing capabilities there, customer base there, supply chain based in Seattle, all make it very attractive to go there, and that is the reason we’re opening up the engineering office,” Mills told GeekWire.

Agile joins a bustling Pacific Northwest aerospace cluster anchored by heavyweights like Blue Origin, SpaceX’s Starlink engineering operations in Redmond, and a growing wave of startups and orbital logistics companies like Starfish Space.

Overall, Agile employs 178 people across the business. The majority of its workforce is based at its Durango headquarters, alongside an additive manufacturing center in Pennsylvania and a 25,000-square-foot test center breaking ground in Tulsa, Okla.

The Bellevue office is located at 3120-3180 139th Ave SE.

Mills noted that the company shares the same concerns recently voiced by Stoke Space CEO Andy Lapsa — who warned during Seattle Space Week in September that if starting over today, “I’m not sure we’d make the same decision” to build in Washington, given local tax and political pressures.

While Agile is eager to tap into the local engineering talent for its Bellevue office, Mills cautioned that future investments, such as a prospective manufacturing and system integration facility, could face headwinds if Washington doesn’t match the aggressive incentives and welcoming posture shown by competing states like Oklahoma.

“I’m just biased towards the opportunity that when you get all the right people in the right place and you have that competition, you have all the capabilities that you need in one place, great things happen,” Mills said. “As people disperse from that, you start to lose that opportunity.”

#4837. Startup Action State launches tools for verifying interactions between AI agents

Action State Group Inc. is launching a set of open-source tools designed to let businesses independently check records of what artificial intelligence agents did, addressing a trust problem as automated systems begin buying, selling and delegating work across corporate boundaries.

The offering combines EvidenceBook, which preserves tamper-evident records, Terms Packs for evaluating agent behavior and Countersign, an independent verification service. The approach targets transactions in which a company cannot rely solely on another party’s account of an agent’s actions.

The fundamental difference between the start-up’s approach and that of observability vendors “is that most of the observability and tracing has to do with what was happening within your own walls,” said Steven Mih, Action State’s founder and chief executive. Mih was previously founder of Ahana Cloud Inc., the developer of a managed version of the Presto open-source distributed query engine. IBM Corp. acquired Ahana in 2023.

EvidenceBook gives participants their own signed transaction histories. A buyer can record what it requested and paid, while a provider records what it accepted and reported delivering. Comparing selected records can reveal agreement, conflicts or gaps without requiring a central authority to hold everyone’s data.

Participants retain control of their information and can disclose additional details when evaluating outcomes.

Both parties need not install the software for one to benefit, although compatible implementations allow them to exchange evidence. Verification can occur offline and is free.

The system uses Agent Action Capsules, an open format for signed records, and cryptographic checkpoints that make subsequent changes detectable. A free public witness establishes that a checkpoint existed by a particular time.

Preserving a record doesn’t establish that its contents are accurate or that every action was captured, Mih said. EvidenceBook verifies that reported events actually occurred but can’t verify that an agreement was satisfied.

That limitation carries through to Countersign. The service checks specified cryptographic and structural properties of an evidence bundle, signs its findings and records them in its own transparency log. It does not certify that claimed outcomes occurred or that the evidence is complete.

Terms Packs address whether recorded work met specified requirements according to regulatory or business rules. Packs define evaluation criteria and methods, with results identifying requirements that were met, missed or could not be evaluated.

An initial pack covers agent-related obligations under the European Union’s AI Act. Action State recommends that governance, risk and compliance teams customize packs to reflect their requirements. The packs do not certify legal compliance.

Human review is critical to the process. Mih described a workflow in which AI judges assess agent records daily, with employees periodically reviewing samples to compare human and automated judgments. The system also preserves evaluation results, allowing auditors to examine the underlying evidence.

Early integrations include Mesh-LLM, an open-source distributed inference network where participating nodes keep signed records and can compare exchanges with peers. The approach could help participants spot changes to a counterparty’s recorded claims about models or configurations.

Project Nanda, an open-source initiative aimed at building infrastructure and protocols for AI agents, has also used Agent Action Capsules in transactions between agents discovered through separate registries. Each side retained a sealed bilateral record. For personal agents, Action State’s Everyday Rules integrates with Muse to evaluate proposed actions against an owner’s requirements.

EvidenceBook is available under the Apache 2.0 license. Its underlying specifications build on published Internet Engineering Task Force standards, but Action State’s own specifications remain individual Internet-Drafts.

“It’s not an RFC standard or adopted by a working group yet,” Mih said, referring to the IETF’s request for comments procedure.

Action State plans to seek neutral foundation governance for the specifications and implementations. The company expects to earn revenue from independent verification services.

“Countersign is currently free,” Mih said. “However, once there is a relying party that accepts the Countersigns, then we will start to charge for that.”

That fingerprint captures patterns that other software can use to distinguish crops, forests, mines and other types of land cover.

With today’s launch, users can use OlmoEarth Embeddings Explorer, a browser-based map-making tool. That means anyone can turn OlmoEarth into real work, from tracking change over time to finding similar places – no sign-in required.

An embedding represents what a model learned from millions of satellite images. OlmoEarth reads a year of radar images from data, including Sentinel-1, Sentinel-2 and Landsat. Sentinel and Landsat are satellite missions that provide complementary views of Earth: Sentinel-1 measures radar reflections, while Sentinel-2 and Landsat capture light in visible and infrared wavelengths.

The company said that, compared with previous generations of the OlmoEarth model, 1.3 is its cheapest model to train. It took 53% of the computing time of the latest version (v1.2) and 18% of the first (v1), with 50% and 22% of the carbon emissions. Critics of the visa programs say tech companies have used them to displace U.S. workers, and that the job ads companies must run before sponsoring workers for green cards often appear in places few American job seekers will see. #4812. Pumpkin Prices at All-Time High—How To Grow Your Own Next Year in Your Backyard

Pumpkins are a fall staple, but unfortunately, their prices have climbed in recent years.

For the week of Oct. 10-22, 2026, grocery stores advertised pumpkins at $5.36 each, compared with $4.83 each in mid-September 2020. That’s an increase of about 11% based on data from the U.S. Department of Agriculture.

Extreme heat and drought, both of which hinder flowering and pollination are two major culprits. Additionally, pumpkins are bulky and expensive to transport, especially when diesel costs are through the roof.

“If a farm loses some of their crop and has to bring in pumpkins from another area, then those added costs, with the high price of fuel, get passed on to the customer,” explains Lindsey Chastain, founder of The Waddle and Cluck in Skiatook, OK.

Fortunately, you don’t have to forgo pumpkins altogether just because prices are rising.

Raghela Scavuzzo, associate director of food systems development at the Illinois Farm Bureau, reinforced to ABC News that diesel prices are a major factor in pumpkin prices, so locally grown pumpkins can give you the most bang for your buck.

Growing your own pumpkins at home can be a good way to prepare for next fall’s festivities while saving some money in the process.

If you have the space, growing a pumpkin patch can help you avoid rising costs once fall 2027 rolls around. Here’s what you’ll need to do:

First, find the ideal pumpkin variety for your space.

If you have a smaller yard, smaller pie pumpkins or more compact varieties are going to be your best bet. However, if you have a big, open, and sunny yard, you may explore larger varieties.

“You can always reach out to your local university extension for guidance,” Chastain says.

It usually takes pumpkins about 85 to 120 days to be ready for harvest. To determine when to plant your pumpkin patch. Chastain suggests counting backward from when you want to harvest.

“Try to plant after the last frost of the season and when the soil is at least around 65 to 70 degrees Fahrenheit so it’s warm enough for the seeds to actually germinate,” advises Chastain.

Location is key if you want to plant a successful patch.. The pumpkins are going to need well-draining soil, a pH of about 5.7 to 6.6, and as much sun as possible.

“Make sure the area you go with doesn't get a lot of standing water. Too much water sitting under your pumpkins after rain can cause them to rot on the bottom,” explains Chastain.

The pumpkin seeds should be at 1 inch deep or up to your first knuckle in the soil. You can usually plant two to three seeds in the same hole and if they all sprout, keep the strongest ones.

Just make sure your pumpkins have plenty of room.

“If you’re planting a traditional vining pumpkin for Halloween next year, you may need 50 to 100 square feet of space per hill, or planting mound, as the vines spread out considerably. If you have a smaller yard, choose a shorter-vine or bush variety that takes up less space,” Chastain says.

Don’t water over the top of the vines or leaves because any water doing so can potentially burn the foliage.

“You want to make sure that you're watering at soil level. Once the flowering starts and the pumpkins start developing, water deeply as they’ll need a pretty good amount of water,” Chastain explains.

Ensure you have pollinator attractors around your pumpkin patch to bring the bees in as they’re what can pollinate the flowers and allow those flowers to turn into pumpkins.

“Having a butterfly garden or bee-friendly flowers close to your pumpkin patch will help pull those bees in and work on the pollination,” says Chastain.

As your vines start growing and spreading, there are some pretty big pests that are going to come and try to attack your vines.

“Look for and get rid of squash bugs, cucumber beetles, and powdery mildew at least once a week. Make sure to check the undersides of leaves and down at the base,” explains Chastain.

Squash bugs can tear through a pumpkin patch pretty quickly once they multiply, and powdery mildew can quickly rot the pumpkin.

Don’t harvest pumpkins until they reach their mature color and their rinds are firm enough that you can’t easily puncture them with a fingernail.

“Cut the stem and leave several inches of that stem attached to the pumpkin and always use clean pruners,” says Chastain.

#4803. To Sell or Rent? How To Choose the Best Path for Your House.

Selling your house is a big step, and you want it to go smoothly. And with houses taking a little longer to sell these days, renting it out might start to sound like a nice backup plan.

But the reality is, while renting can work well for some people, it’s also a big commitment. So before you decide, it helps to see the whole picture first.

Lately, some sellers are choosing to hang on to their houses and rent them out instead. According to Parcl, around 9% of homes for rent right now were previously up for sale.

Some homeowners put their houses on the market expecting a faster process than they got or a certain price buyers didn’t respond to. And when things didn’t go exactly as planned, they decided to rent instead. As Kara Ng, Senior Economist at Zillow, explains:

“Bargaining power is tilting toward buyers and homes are taking longer to sell, making renting out a property one way to buy time rather than compete aggressively on price.”

But here’s the important distinction. Just because some sellers are choosing to rent doesn’t mean selling isn’t working. In many cases, the difference comes down to how well a house is priced, prepared, and marketed for today’s buyers.

Parcl data shows where these “accidental landlords” – owners who planned to sell but decided to rent instead – are most common (see graph below):

They make up around 17% of homes for rent in places like Sherman and Houston, TX, but less than 4% in markets like Des Moines, IA and Bloomington, IN.

That’s a pretty big difference. And it shows why you have to know what’s happening in your neighborhood. Your market, your asking price, and your strategy all play a role in how your sale goes.

A local agent can show you how houses like yours are selling, what strategies work best, and help you weigh your options – not to push you to sell, but so you at least have the full picture based on today’s market. And part of making an informed decision is understanding what renting your house would actually take.

While renting might sound like easy passive income on paper, in practice, it’s a hands-on job with rules to follow. As Invest West Management puts it:

“Even if you never intended to become a landlord, the law still sees you as one — and you must meet all legal obligations that come with that role. This often includes registering the tenancy, providing tenants with certain disclosures, and ensuring the property meets safety standards.”

You’d also be the one chasing missed rent payments, fixing damage between tenants, and covering unexpected repairs. Not to mention fielding weekend calls about leaky dishwashers or broken AC units.

There are expenses beyond regular maintenance and repairs, too. According to Bankrate, these are a few of the biggest:

Insurance premiums (landlord insurance typically costs about 25% more than a standard homeowners policy)

Management fees (if you hire a property manager, they usually charge around 10% of the rent)

Gaps between tenants, when you pay the mortgage without rental income

If you want to be a landlord and you’re prepared for the responsibility, renting may be a great fit. But if you’d really rather sell, don’t let today’s market convince you that you can’t.

A great agent can make it happen. The key is having an open conversation up front with your agent about pricing and how to market your house. Nailing those details from day 1 helps your house make a great first impression, so renting doesn’t have to be your fallback.

Renting can be a good fit for the right person with the right house, but it isn’t the only option.
