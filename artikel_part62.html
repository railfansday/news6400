#3348. Beyond Good & Evil 2 reportedly still in development 18 years after being announced, now a "solo-first" adventure

"Its combat is very reminiscent of Insomniac's Spider-Man games."

Beyond Good & Evil 2 is reportedly still in pre-production a staggering 18 years after it was first announced, and it has apparently evolved from being a multiplayer-first project to being "solo-first" and story based, with additional, optional co-op.

Origami Media (in a French report translated by DeepL) said it heard from several people close to the game's development that the idea for Beyond Good and Evil 2, in 2019-2022, was for the game to be "highly multiplayer and connected", featuring "a multitude of planets" where players would be matchmade and play together. Sound familiar? It's supposed to.

"The key concept was the 'third place' - a spot where you might hang out with mates after work, just as you would in Fortnite or Destiny," Origami shared, "which were cited as sources of inspiration. You played as a blank character, tagging along on the adventures of the heroes of Beyond Good & Evil, in the passenger seat."

But since then, the project "evolved", from smaller matchmade groups to more open-ended play, then to team-based play in groups of four and finally, to pairs. "The aim now is to offer a 'solo-first' adventure, with no mandatory online connection and featuring a strong narrative that can also be played in co-op," Origami stated.

"A few years ago, some people thought it bore a resemblance to Zelda in space; today, its combat is very reminiscent of Insomniac's Spider-Man games."

Yet despite its incredible length of time in development - I was at the Paris-based UbiDays event in 2008 when Ubisoft revealed the first CGI trailer for Beyond Good and Evil 2 - the game is apparently yet to enter full production. "If we go by the terminology used at Ubisoft and in many other major companies in the sector," Origami went on, "production of BGE2 has not yet begun. It is currently in pre-production, which still involves at least 250 people (at Ubisoft Montpellier alone). And this project is now aiming to move into the alpha phase."

According to the report, "Historically, BGE2 has failed to meet all its creative milestones." These are referred to as "gates", which sound like playable builds of the game, and it kept missing these because ideas about the game kept changing. "Is the game cool enough? Innovative? Can we make this loop more engaging? Hang on, let's push the deadline back again - we could improve the combat system... and so on," Origami wrote.

The reason the unfinished game survived so long was because it had "unwavering" support from Ubisoft. "It was impossible for it to be cancelled," Origami added. "To the point where it became something of a poison, according to several people within the company. If the project is never cancelled, could it also never be finished?"

But wide-ranging restructuring at Ubisoft, which most recently caused the closure of Trials developer RedLynx and the downsizing of the Assassin's Creed Black Flag Resynced team in Ubisoft Singapore - and reportedly layoffs at Germany offices too - has pulled that rug of invincibility from under the project. "They are now being told, rather more matter-of-factly: 'We don't know what's going to happen.'" One source said this was a "return to a sense of reality".

Beyond Good and Evil 2 holds the record for the video game longest in development, having surpassed Duke Nukem Forever's 14-year record in 2022. The game came closest to being realised, seemingly, in around 2018, when Ubisoft showed Beyond Good and Evil 2 to press - including Eurogamer - behind closed doors at E3, and promised a playable beta towards the end of 2019. But that never materialised. More recently, the game got a new creative director, and job adverts for the project have been sporadically appearing, including one spotted the other day.

In August, Amy Helm—the daughter of late Band member Levon Helm, and a singer-songwriter in her own right—was sued in federal court for self-dealing, trademark infringement, and causing $2 million in damages to Levon Helm Studios (LHS), among other charges. Amy Helm has now countersued the studio, alleging that her successor, Brian Parillo, used invalid corporate actions to take control of the famed music venue in Woodstock, New York.

In May, Amy Helm was removed as president of LHS by her stepmother and Levon's widow, Sandra Helm, as well as LHS shareholders Parillo and Barbara O’Brien, over allegations of mismanagement. In response, Amy Helm accused Parillo of financial exploitation and elder abuse of Sandra Helm in a June court filing; Parillo denied those charges. There was also a physical dispute between Parillo and Amy Helm after her removal, during which police were called to LHS. No charges were filed.

In a new 34-page complaint filed on October 16 in New York Supreme Court, Amy Helm alleges that Parillo took control of LHS by way of voting assignments. Amy and Sandra Helm both own 40 percent of the company, while Parillo and O’Brien each own 10 percent. The lawsuit states that Parillo claimed voting assignments from Sandra Helm and O’Brien—thus receiving a purported 60 percent of the company’s power despite owning only 10 percent of its shares—and declared himself director, president, secretary, and treasurer of LHS. Amy Helm’s attorneys state she never signed any documents consenting to that change, that no shareholder meeting was held, and that his takeover is void.

The lawsuit also claims Parillo’s actions amount to “undue influence” over Sandra Helm, citing her sworn affirmation in a separate dispute over Levon Helm’s estate, in which her stepmother allegedly said, “I thought, ‘they’ve conned me.‘ I’m so gullible, they’re taking advantage of me. Pitchfork has reached out to representatives for Amy Helm and Levon Helm Studios for comment.

Back in August, LHS claimed Amy Helm charged $400,000 to the studio to cover personal expenses, took an additional $200,000 from the Levon Helm Estate (including from an LLC that distributes royalties from Levon’s final albums, thus blocking the payment to those who worked on the records), and infringed on the Midnight Ramble name—a concert series her father hosted at LHS, which was continued under her leadership following his death—by creating her own trademark, Helm Family Midnight Ramble, and then diverting the revenue into her own company.

In an interview with the Overlook this summer, Sandra Helm responded to her stepdaughter’s early claims, stating, “I didn’t like that they wanted to portray Brian like some kind of a puppet master or Svengali that came in and overtook my mind. #3345. EA Shuts Down Dragon Age, Mass Effect Studio BioWare

Publisher Electronic Arts is planning to shut down legendary RPG studio BioWare, laying off about 50 employees at the studio alongside 350 more elsewhere.

In a new Bloomberg report, a person familiar with the matter said a total of 3% of EA's overall company has been affected by this latest round of layoffs.

BioWare posted a statement to X, confirming that it's shutting down and thanking everyone for their support over the studio's lifetime. It's quite a sad day.

"There is nothing easy about writing these words," BioWare said. "Our focus right now is on our incredibly talented team who put their hearts into these games, and supporting one another through this transition. What generations of BioWare built together is something to celebrate and remember. These games became so much more because you played them, debated your choices, fell in love with the characters, and made these worlds your own."

In a staff letter sent out on Wednesday that has now been shared by the company, EA chief operating officer David Tinson and chief studios officer Cam Weber explained that the company took a "hard look" at its business over the last several months. This resulted in some projects getting canceled, including the next Mass Effect game--which was in early development at BioWare--as well as a planned game in The Sims franchise codenamed Project Lotus. Instead, the resources allocated to that title will be diverted to something else currently codenamed Project Evergreen.

Many of EA's currently released franchises will continue as expected. Full Circle will keep working on Skate, but EA said the team there would be downsized. Meanwhile, its myriad sports games, Apex Legends, Battlefield, the next Star Wars Jedi game, and a new game based on Iron Man will continue to be invested in.

"Across our portfolio, we're making deliberate choices to align talent and investments where we see the greatest opportunities for players and long-term growth," Tinson and Weber wrote in the letter.

This comes one year after EA agreed to a buyout by Saudi Arabia's Public Investment Fund for $55 billion and two months after the company went private following the acquisition. The writing was on the wall, however, as EA told investors in March that it planned to save some $700 million after the massive leveraged buyout saddled the company with billions of dollars in debt.

The studio needs no introduction, but BioWare is quite the storied developer. Founded in February 1995, the Canadian studio is known for its many RPGs from Baldur's Gate and Star Wars: Knights of the Old Republic to Dragon Age and Mass Effect. Things haven't been too successful for BioWare recently, though, with both 2019's Anthem and 2024's Dragon Age: The Veilguard failing to meet expectations. While Mass Effect 5 has been in development since at least February 2022, that project is dead ... and so is BioWare.

AllTrails is a popular tool for finding walking, hiking, running, and biking routes around the world. You can use it to locate trails and check user reviews, but the app has plenty of features for customizing activities, tracking conditions, and navigating (safely) in real time. Here are the tips and tricks to get the most out of AllTrails.

The elevation map is useful for visualizing total gain and loss on your route, but you can scrub the chart on mobile to see exactly where climbs or obstacles are on the map. For example, you can see where the grade increases (and what the percentage is) or how far you are from stream crossings or switchbacks. Open the map from the trail page or the Navigate function and drag your finger across the elevation profile to track the location marker on the map.

You may be most likely to search for trails based on length and difficulty as well as location, but AllTrails has a wealth of filters to find routes with specific features, or that are dog- or kid-friendly. You can also use filters to identify trails that are likely to be less crowded (though if a route is on AllTrails, it certainly isn't a secret). In the Search window, tap All to open available filters. Select Not very busy under Visitation and Loop under Route type (out-and-back trails can feel busier). Select a rating and add any other requirements, then hit Show trails.

When you use AllTrails to log an activity or write a review, a map of your track is saved to the trail listing and visible to other users. You can disable this in Settings > Privacy settings > Content—but even if you don't display your activity, you can use those that are public to find custom routes that string multiple trails together or add a ridgeline not included in the official AllTrails track. Scroll down to the reviews section and tap the activities link to pull up maps of completed activities. Note, though, that some variations in routes may not be actual detours, and you should still follow Leave No Trace principles and stay within your skill level when it comes to difficulty.

A free AllTrails account will get you most of the service's basic features, but you have to pay for a Plus subscription—$35.99 per year—for things like offline maps and wrong-turn alerts. If you have an REI membership, you get a 30% discount on paid AllTrails subscriptions, bringing the annual fee down to $24.99 per year. (A Peak subscription that usually costs $79.99 per year drops to $49.99 per year.) In the AllTrails app, go to your profile > Settings > REI Membership and follow the steps to connect your account and claim the discount.

User reviews can give you a good sense of current trail conditions, such as seasonal closures and whether there are muddy or snowy conditions, as well as more global environmental considerations like bugs and river crossings. You should obviously read the most recent reviews for your route, but you can also keyword search on desktop (CTRL/CMD + F) for terms like "mud," "scramble," "snow," "gate," "bugs," or "ticks." A trail may be marked as "easy," but this can reveal issues like snowmelt that have made running water trickier to wade through. You can read the reviews from the same month in previous years too.

When you search trails and trailheads by name on popular mapping apps, you may not always get directions to the exact starting location pinned on AllTrails. Instead, launch directions from the AllTrails listing to use the GPS coordinates, which you can then search in Google Maps or Apple Maps. On the trail page, scroll down to Get directions, then select your preferred map app (or copy the coordinates to the clipboard).

When you record an activity in AllTrails, you have the option to add a waypoint, which is essentially a note pinned to a specific location on the route. This can be useful if you want to remember details, such as tricky turns or hidden features, and you can opt into adding your waypoints to community layers to help other users. Open the Navigator in the mobile app and tap the arrow in the lower-right corner. Swipe up and tap Add Waypoint. Select the waypoint type, add details and select privacy settings, and tap Save.

#3301. How I'm Using the Time Change to Kickstart My Morning Exercise Routine

I look forward to the fall time change every year, because I have plans for that extra morning hour. Turning back the clock is a perfect opportunity to kick-start a morning routine, since you can get up an hour early without it feeling like you're getting up an hour early.

We are, unfortunately, still doing clock changes twice a year. But I will take advantage of that fact while I can, because the fall time change gifts us with an extra hour in the morning, even if it is cruelly ripped from our evening routines. I'm not a morning person by nature, but my day always goes better when I get my workout in early, so I'm going to take that extra morning hour and use it for a workout instead of recalibrating my body clock to wake up later. I recommend all my fellow night owls do the same.

I mean, you could use your extra hour to sleep in—take it if you need it. But personally, I'm going to set my alarm an hour earlier than I get up, starting on Nov. 1, the first day of standard time for 2026. An hour is enough time for a simple strength workout or for a short run and a quick shower before officially starting your day. I reserve one day a week for sleeping in, which for me is Saturday. On days I'm resting from exercise, I'll still wake up on schedule but use my morning hour for something fun or relaxing, like reading a book.

If you want to fit a full workout in and need more time than the hour gives you, this is still a good way to soften the blow. For example, you can wake up 90 minutes earlier (according to the clock) with it only feeling 30 minutes earlier than usual (according to your circadian rhythms). I've been using my extra hour a little differently each year, depending on how committed I am to those morning workouts. This year, I may split it between extra sleep and a mini workout.

On the first day you wake up "early," things will be much as they always are. But now that you're becoming a morning exerciser, you'll have to come to terms with the winter darkness. It will creep in, sunrise getting a few minutes later each day, until your morning jog is, most likely, fully in the dark.

If you are running in the early mornings, definitely get yourself a light to help you see and be seen. I have a Petzl Tikkina, which I bought a few years back because Petzl has a reputation for reliability, and the Tikkina is one of their more affordable models. It's worked so well that the second winter I used it, I sprang for the rechargeable battery that fits in place of its three AAA cells. The rechargeable is designed to work well even in cold conditions, which is a nice plus for winter runs. Also consider wearing bright colors, a reflective vest, or even something like the Noxgear Tracer to give you good visibility on the side of the road. I have a neon pink jacket that comes out on cold dark mornings.

At the gym, be aware that the rhythm of the place will change. There will be a different group of regulars, and it may be more or less busy than you're used to. But even if everything is the same, there’s something luxurious about being able to take your time, when normally you have to squeeze your last few exercises together and rush to work. Savor that extra hour.
