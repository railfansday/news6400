#1645. Coventry vs. Fulham: Head-to-head record and past meetings

By Oliver Thomas | 22 Oct 2026 21:45

Coventry City and Fulham lock horns at the CBS Arena for gameweek seven in the Premier League on Saturday afternoon.

Frank Lampard's side were unable to record a third successive top-flight victory as they fell to a 3-1 away defeat against Tottenham Hotspur last Monday.

As for the Cottagers, they celebrated their first Premier League win of the season under new head coach Alvaro Arbeloa last weekend, narrowly beating Hull City 1-0 on home soil.

Ahead of this contest between two teams sitting in the bottom four of the table, Sports Mole takes an in-depth look at the head-to-head record and previous meetings between the two sides.

Coventry City and Fulham have played against each other a total of 39 times across all competitions and it is the Cottagers who marginally lead the head-to-head record with 16 wins to the Sky Blues' 14, while nine draws have also been played out.

October 1919 was the first time these two sides butted heads in the old Division Two, with Fulham securing a slender 1-0 away victory; this was the first of four wins posted by the Cottagers in their opening five encounters with Coventry.

The Sky Blues responded by winning three of their next four league meetings, including their very first against Fulham in September 1921 (2-0 at home).

Between 1929 and 1932, Fulham and Coventry competed in the third division of English football. The Cottagers won three, drew three and lost two of their eight matches. A eventful 5-3 home win for Fulham was secured in August 1931, just five months before the two teams shared the spoils in a thrilling 5-5 draw.

Over a 13-year period up until 1949, Fulham and Coventry played 12 times against each other in the old Division Two and it was the Sky Blues who had the greater share of success, winning seven and drawing one while the Cottagers only registered four victories.

Almost two decades later, the two teams faced off in the 1967-68 Division One campaign, with Fulham collecting four points from their home (1-1 draw) and away (3-0 win) fixtures.

The last time Coventry and Fulham faced off in the league was during the 2021-22 Championship season when the Cottagers secured promotion as champions.

However, Marco Silva's Fulham lost home and away to Mark Robins's Coventry, who secured a 4-1 triumph at the CBS Arena - their joint biggest-ever win over the Cottagers - before celebrating a 3-1 success at Craven Cottage to complete their first league double over the West Londoners for 74 years.

Away from league action, Coventry and Fulham have only ever faced each other three times in domestic cup competitions. Their one and only FA Cup meeting took place in November 1928 when Fulham ran away 4-1 away victors in the first round.

These two teams have also squared off in one EFL Cup tie in October 1982, with Coventry progressing from the second round via the away goals rule after a 2-2 draw was followed by a goalless stalemate in the replay.

Every time one set of fans are caught and an issue blows up, the other side’s keyboard warriors respond with: ‘Aye, but what about when your lot did such and such?’ It is as embarrassing as it is predictable.

That whataboutery is part of the disease. It turns every incident into a points-scoring exercise. Nothing ever changes.

The same problems exist now as they did 30, 40, 50 years ago. The same songs are wheeled out, the same excuses are made, and the idiots continue to tarnish the matchday experience for everyone else.

All of which begs a very simple question. Why are some football fans seemingly incapable of just showing basic human decency?

Celtic held a tribute to their former winger Johnny Doyle, who died in 1981

Kids who attend these matches and witness these things are, in some cases, brainwashed to believe that abuse of the most disgusting kind is simply a performative aspect of the matchday experience as a fan.

Scottish football has spent years pretending this is an issue that will eventually somehow fade away. It has not. The numbers involved may be small relative to the overall support, but they are loud.

They are organised and emboldened by the knowledge that their clubs and the authorities have limited appetite for the sustained confrontation required to root them out.

Supporters who also feel embarrassed when some of their own choose to indulge in similar bile. All of this comes at a time when the football authorities are finally showing some backbone in their efforts to tackle fan disorder.

Handing out stadium bans is one thing. But as Geissler has demonstrated, a large part of this also comes down to self-policing among supporters. Everyone has a duty to call it out and do better.

#1603. Doncaster deserves praise for seeking to maximise SPFL's next TV deal ... but can we trust him to negotiate it?

By CALUM CROWE, SCOTTISH DEPUTY CHIEF SPORTS WRITER

Published: 18:08 BST, 20 October 2026 | Updated: 18:12 BST, 20 October 2026

When the Scottish Professional Football League (SPFL) renewed their TV deal with Sky Sports last time around, they came under a lot of flak. Quite rightly, too.

The league chose not to open it out to competitive tender. There was no formal bidding process. Instead, just a cosy continuation of the status quo.

It was only natural to wonder whether it was really a good deal at all. Some insiders at the time reckoned the SPFL could have left £20million on the table by failing to invite bids.

Stewart Robertson, the former Rangers managing director who used to serve on the SPFL board, didn't mince his words when he spoke about this a few years ago.

Speaking even before that last deal had been announced, Robertson took aim at SPFL chief executive Neil Doncaster and claimed that Scottish football was being under-sold.

The SPFL have done the right thing this time. They have opened it up and invited broadcasters to bid for the rights to show Scottish Premiership matches from 2029-30 onwards.

All of this is welcome. But it also relies on Doncaster's skills as a negotiator to maximise value not only for the clubs and the league as a whole, but also to ensure fans don't end up being fleeced.

Neil Doncaster has been criticised for not bringing in more TV revenue for Scottish football

Because that's the danger. If more broadcasters start piling in and want a piece of the pie, it will almost inevitably lead to fans having to fork out for more subscriptions.

It is a difficult balancing act. Doncaster has never come across as a strong and robust negotiator at any point whilst in charge of the SPFL.

Fans are already having to pay for multiple subscriptions with Sky Sports and Premier Sports, along with the cost of season tickets. All of this at a time when there is a cost-of-living crisis.

Opening the process to tender was overdue and correct. Maximising the money is essential if the league is to grow and clubs are to prosper.

But if the only model on offer is one that continually loads extra cost on to fans, then the SPFL will have solved one problem by creating a bigger one.
