T-Mobile has come to an agreement with the New York State Attorney General’s Office over claims that the carrier did not honor price guarantees for T-Mobile One plans on its 4G network between January 2017 and April 2019. Those plans were advertised as being “Price Guarantee Plans.”

Affected active T-Mobile customers who leave the carrier will be able to collect a credit worth the last month’s payment and a $25 gift card.

Those who stay on and are still using an older phone can get a free 5G phone (worth up to $250) or credit up to $150 toward a more expensive 5G phone for lines that are still using 3G or 4G. Rates for their recurring monthly plans will stay the same until 2029.

It also pegs the relief to be worth around $38 million in value.

“T-Mobile misled hundreds of thousands of New Yorkers by promising that it would never raise prices on their cellphone plans, only to do exactly that just a few years later,” said James in the release. “Consumers shouldn’t have to pay the price for a misleading sale.”

The office’s filed agreement notes that T-Mobile made the price guarantee in at least two press releases and during a CNET video interview with then-CEO John Legere in 2017. We’re glad to have this matter resolved and remain focused on delivering value to our customers.”

Per the agreement, T-Mobile has 150 days starting Oct. 20 to identify and notify customers who are eligible for the relief. For the latter, T-Mobile will also not charge any costs such as transfer or activation fees.

Also: 5 ways to improve your Chrome browser’s security

In a blog post published Tuesday, Google outlined three new measures designed to help Chrome users browse the web more safely and securely. The new measures apply to Chrome both on computers and mobile devices.

First up is protection against suspicious websites. Chrome has long warned you about dangerous sites that are known to be malicious. Now, the browser will also notify you if a site is deemed suspicious. The warning that appears on the screen may specifically tell you that the site doesn’t seem right, as it’s similar to other sites associated with phishing, malware, and scams. You can click a Learn More link to delve further.

If you’re concerned about the site and want to follow Google’s advice, just close it or click the Return to safety button to go back to the previous page. If you’re certain the site is safe, you can mark it as safe and use it normally.

Google rates a website as suspicious by comparing it with other deceptive or misleading sites. Gemini also plays a role here, as the AI analyzes the site’s content, the brand it represents, the site’s intent, the domain’s reputation, and other factors to derive a scam likelihood score.

With this update, Chrome can now distinguish between suspicious sites and dangerous sites by flashing different warnings. The alert for a dangerous site paints the screen red and strongly recommends that you leave the page. The alert for a suspicious site is more subtle, giving you the option to still visit and use the site if you’re certain it’s legit.

“Suspicious site warnings notify users when a page exhibits deceptive patterns commonly associated with known malicious sites,” Google said in its blog post. “These new warnings are distinguished by format, iconography, color, and text, to make it easy for users to quickly and confidently make the best choice for themselves based on the context. By tailoring the warning UI in this way, we are able to safely expand our coverage to display a higher volume of protective warnings.”

Google has also beefed up Chrome’s overall scam-detection capabilities. Last year, the company added Gemini’s AI smarts to Chrome to thwart tech support scams. Here, the scammer behind a malicious site tries to convince you that you’ve been infected by malware, only to try to sell you security software or gain remote access to your computer.

Now, this same type of AI-powered security will alert you against a wider range of online scams, including fake antivirus and phony browser updates. For this, Google uses a server-side Gemini model with more advanced capabilities. The model analyzes the content of the page, the reputation of the domain, and other signals to reach a final conclusion.

Also: How Google’s AI combats new scam tactics

To protect your privacy, the company promises that the amount of data shared with the back-end server is minimized and is shared only when the site triggers high levels of scam signals. Any page content sent from your computer or mobile device isn’t logged, remembered, or used to train the AI, Google added.

ClickFix is a type of social engineering scam that tricks you into manually copying and running malicious commands on your own computer, ostensibly to fix a technical issue that doesn’t exist. Because you carry out the actual steps yourself, existing security defenses in Chrome and other products won’t catch or block the attack, at least until now.

With the latest update, Chrome will now detect if you’re tricked into copying suspicious commands. After you copy them but before you have a chance to paste them, the clipboard is wiped of any personal data. Chrome then analyzes that data and displays a warning if the activity is deemed dangerous.

To take advantage of the new security defenses in Chrome, you need to be running the browser in Enhanced Protection mode. This is the strongest level available in the program’s Safe Browsing process. To enable this in Chrome on the desktop or mobile, head to Settings and select Privacy and security. On the desktop, click the option for Security; on mobile, tap the option for Safe Browsing. Then choose the setting for Enhanced Protection.

You should also turn on Enhanced protection for your Google account. For this, sign in to your Google account page on the web. Select the option for Security & sign-in. Scroll down the page and select the option for Enhanced Safe Browsing for your account. Then just turn on the switch.

And with Microsoft continually expanding its quantum computing technologies and already integrating them into its Azure platform, Microsoft is in a unique position to benefit from two of the most significant technologies to emerge in decades.

The findings raise questions about whether researchers fully understand how to effectively control agents.

Unlike recent security incidents, where agents explicitly breached systems, examples like these show AI agents suppressing one of the most crucial ways developers can track their behavior. The capability itself is what’s worth noticing here, as is the timeline for OpenAI’s discovery and reporting of it.

The company isn’t sure why agents did this, but said it was possibly due to “benign overpersistence,” a trait that models have to complete tasks at any cost, often to the detriment of established security layers, without understanding safety as a priority. This behavior has been a theme in recent breaches and helps explain why models cheat at tasks.

OpenAI explained that it’s easier for models to disable monitoring in evaluations than in Codex workloads, for example, since agents are separated from monitoring in Codex, and the company doesn’t “require misalignment monitoring when evaluating models comparable to GPT-6 Luna, given their weaker capabilities.” But classifying safety approaches based on what developers believe a model’s capabilities are doesn’t look like a very foolproof approach.

Applying with a co-signer can make you a stronger candidate in a lender's eyes. Co-signer release removes your co-signer from legal responsibility for your loan. You'll likely want to release your co-signer once you're able to better manage the loan on your own.

To qualify for co-signer release, you'll typically need to make a set number of consecutive, on-time payments, often between 12 and 48. Your lender may also require you to undergo a hard credit check. Each lender sets its own requirements for co-signer release, so make sure you understand the terms before applying.

A longer waiting period, such as the 48 months required when refinancing with a lender like Yrefy, may be fine if you think you'll need that time to improve your finances or income. However, your co-signer may want to be released sooner.

Not every lender offers co-signer release, so check before applying to make sure the option is available. For example, SoFi doesn't offer co-signer release on refinanced loans, so if release is important to you or your co-signer, make sure the lender offers it before applying.

If the primary borrower dies, the loan may be discharged, but this depends on the lender and the loan terms. Before agreeing to co-sign, find out what happens if the primary borrower dies, since the co-signer could still be on the hook.

A bond investor who buys a bond at issuance knows immediately: Here's my cost, here's what I'll earn and here's when my initial investment will be returned. When investors sell bonds to each other, things get more interesting, because the market now determines the price, and that shifts the relative value of the yield.

Here's how that works. If you bought a $100 bond with $5 annual yield payments, that yield is 5%. If you have binoculars you'll also see the Endymion Crater, the Alphonsus Crater, and the Clavius Crater. Plus, if you have a telescope, you'll also see the Linne Crater, Rima Ariadaeus, and the Apollo 17 landing spot.

The next Full Moon will take place on October 25 or 26, depending on your timezone.

The Moon takes around 29.5 days to travel around Earth, and its appearance changes throughout the month. As it moves around our planet, we see different amounts of its sunlit side, from a slim crescent to the quarter phases and eventually a Full Moon. It then begins the cycle again. As NASA explains, these phases are caused by the Moon's changing position relative to Earth and the Sun.

New Moon - The Moon is between Earth and the sun, so the side we see is dark (in other words, it's invisible to the eye).

Waxing Crescent - A small sliver of light appears on the right side (Northern Hemisphere).

First Quarter - Half of the Moon is lit on the right side. It looks like a half-Moon.

Waxing Gibbous - More than half is lit up, but it's not quite full yet.

Full Moon - The whole face of the Moon is illuminated and fully visible.

Waning Gibbous - The Moon starts losing light on the right side. (Northern Hemisphere)

Third Quarter (or Last Quarter) - Another half-Moon, but now the left side is lit.

Waning Crescent - A thin sliver of light remains on the left side before going dark again.
