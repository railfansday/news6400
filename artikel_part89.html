#1999. Celtic's rampant, ruthless display against a so-called title rival is just what Martin O'Neill needed

Published: 20:51 BST, 18 October 2026 | Updated: 21:16 BST, 18 October 2026

If Martin O’Neill had any lingering doubts as to whether he should have stayed on as Celtic manager, this will surely have eliminated them.

The veteran boss took a few days to weigh up his future during the international break, presumably wondering whether he had the appetite for all this at the age of 74.

But he decided to remain, and a devastating 7-1 win against Hearts, still one of Celtic’s closest challengers, will have convinced him that he was right.

Let’s not pretend that everything is suddenly rosy at a club who are coming up woefully short in Europe and contriving to infuriate supporters with their incompetence off the pitch.

But a brutal demolition of the team who pushed them so close last season was exactly the kind of statement win O’Neill needed to invigorate his squad and maintain a semblance of unity in and around Parkhead.

One thing’s for sure: nobody will be complaining if there is more of this in the weeks ahead. Celtic were ruthless, but they were also hungry and relentless all over the pitch.

Benjamin Nygren celebrates after scoring Celtic's second goal in the 7-1 thrashing of Hearts

Kieran Tierney netted an early opener before having an outstanding game at Celtic Park

Callum Osmand celebrates with fellow striker Camilo Duran after scoring Celtic's third goal

Some suggested later that it was reminiscent of the side under Ange Postecoglou, which will be music to the ears of Dermot Desmond as he seeks respite from the critics.

O’Neill, of course, is just as popular as the big Australian, but he has been searching for something this season, unsure as to the quality of his new signings and the best way to disguise his team’s faults.

Might he have stumbled on a winning formula? Prompted by successful substitutions made in the midweek defeat by Benfica, O’Neill made changes that worked a treat, most notably a first start for Callum Osmand.

Quick and direct, the young striker inspired Celtic’s breathless attack. Wide on the left is not his favoured position, but he was full of energy and ambition, certainly more than Sebastian Tounekti has offered in the same role.

Osmand, of course, was the player who completed the scoring on that momentous final day of last season when two late goals in a 3-1 win over Hearts earned Celtic a thrilling title triumph.

Here, his two goals in the space of two minutes underpinned a rampant first-half performance by Celtic. His first was a cool finish after calmly opening his body, his second a close-range header.

On the other side, Haissem Hassan was also given his chance. The Egyptian winger adds another dimension to Celtic’s attack, as he showed with one delightful spin away from his marker that resulted in a run and shot off the post.

Young Colby Donovan played his heart out at right-back, Kieran Tierney returned to his best and Camilo Duran showed his class when he peeled off Stephen Kingsley and curled in Celtic’s sixth. With the Colombia striker thriving at centre-forward, record signing Kasper Hogh can have no complaints about watching all this from the bench.

As ever, though, a degree of perspective is in order. If Celtic’s recent struggles in Europe are any guide, it will be a different story on Thursday night when Celta Vigo come calling. Dane Murray will be given a proper test at centre-half, Hassan will have to do some of the defending that he patently doesn’t fancy and the team as a whole will have to find a way of shoring up centre midfield.

What’s more, it would be a mistake to get carried away with this comprehensive dismantling of hopeless opponents. If Hearts’ intention was to go to Celtic Park and prove that they can challenge once again for the title, they failed in the most humiliating fashion. Rarely has a team purporting to be potential champions made such a feeble case.

It wasn’t so much that they were outclassed in every department (statistics, rather bizarrely, show that they had more possession and more attempts on goal than Celtic). It was that they lacked character. That they were physically and mentally weak. That they were hopelessly naive – as players in their individual duels and as a team.

Wouter Vrancken has been hailed for his work as head coach this season, but boy does he have some thinking to do – about his personnel, his tactics and how best to combine the two. This looked for all the world like a Hearts team who are not nearly as good as either they or their manager thought they were. A team and leader who had forgotten about the fundamentals.

In the first half of a ridiculously open game, it beggared belief how often Hearts’ players were left exposed at the back, victims of their casual team-mates and an over-optimistic game plan. They were half-hearted in the tackle, loose with the ball and, most worrying of all, unable to see the error of their ways. Not until they were 6-1 down did Vrancken pull off wingers Josh McPake and Calvin Miller. Never again will Celtic have it so easy against a so-called title rival.

Still, they are to be commended for taking full advantage with a barnstorming display that will boost everyone at Celtic Park. With Rangers only able to draw with Dundee on Saturday, O’Neill’s side are now four points clear of their city rivals.

They are also eight ahead of third-placed Hearts, although you wonder about the relevance of that statistic now. It may well be that this season’s title race extends into the final 11 minutes of the final game, but if it does, don’t expect the Edinburgh side to be involved.

#1998. Augsburg’s Manuel Baum left craving duller football after weeks of Bundesliga spectacles

FC Augsburg may be struggling to secure Bundesliga victories at present, but Manuel Baum’s team are certainly providing plenty of entertainment. The Fuggerstädter have scored at least twice in all six of their opening league fixtures, establishing a new club record. Unfortunately, their remarkable attacking output has not translated into a particularly impressive points return.

Augsburg have amassed just eight points despite scoring 16 goals, a total bettered only by Bayern Munich’s 22. Four consecutive matches without victory have left Baum facing questions about his team’s defensive stability ahead of Saturday’s home meeting with Union Berlin.

“If we win matches, there can be as much spectacle as anyone wants,” Baum said at Thursday’s press conference. “But I have to say there was way too much spectacle in Elversberg. But I guess for outsiders, it’s a lot of fun to watch our matches because there’s always something going on. For the outside observers, it’s a lot of fun. I personally could do with a bit more calm in some situations.”

The contrast with the opening weeks of the campaign is striking. Following an extraordinary comeback victory against Eintracht Frankfurt in September, Augsburg celebrated their first-ever appearance at the top of the Bundesliga table. Baum was sufficiently delighted to take a screenshot of the standings, explaining that the historic moment deserved to be remembered even if his team could not remain at the summit indefinitely.

The goals have continued to flow, but Augsburg’s inability to protect leads has become an increasingly serious concern. Baum’s players have conceded 11 goals across their four-match winless sequence, including five in the final ten minutes of matches this season.

They have surrendered advantages against Bayer Leverkusen, Bayern Munich, and Werder Bremen. The Bavarian Swabians have actually led in five of their six Bundesliga fixtures, with last weekend’s extraordinary encounter against SV Elversberg providing the sole exception.

That particular match illustrated the entertaining and occasionally bewildering nature of Augsburg’s campaign. Elversberg’s 4-3 victory featured seven goals, four penalties, and a succession of controversial VAR interventions. Baum’s team came within touching distance of recovering from a three-goal deficit before ultimately departing the Saarland empty-handed.

The chaotic contest left players and coaches struggling to process everything that had happened. Baum himself expressed frustration with the officiating, particularly the failure to award what he considered a clear late penalty. The defeat nevertheless highlighted a broader problem that cannot simply be attributed to refereeing decisions.

Augsburg continue to create chances and score goals, but their defensive vulnerabilities have repeatedly undermined otherwise promising performances.

For spectators, Baum’s Augsburg have rarely been dull. The coach would undoubtedly prefer a little less excitement in front of his own goal.

#1989. Hamburg injuries clear as Merlin Polzin targets fourth consecutive Bundesliga victory

Hamburg head coach Merlin Polzin has received a welcome personnel boost ahead of Saturday’s Bundesliga trip to SC Paderborn. Patson Daka, Albert Sambi Lokonga, and Bakery Jatta are all available again after recovering from their respective injury problems. The returning trio provide the 35-year-old with additional options as Hamburger SV attempt to secure a fourth consecutive Bundesliga victory.

Speaking at Thursday’s pre-match press conference, Polzin confirmed that the three players had participated extensively in training during the week, with some completing full sessions. Captain Nicolai Remberg and the suspended Mario Vuskovic remain unavailable, but the coach was otherwise pleased with his squad’s improving fitness situation.

The welcome developments nevertheless present Polzin with something of a selection dilemma. Following HSV’s dramatic 1-0 victory over VfB Stuttgart last weekend, the Hanseaten have now won three consecutive Bundesliga matches for the first time in 16 years.

“For us, this is an outstanding situation to be in,” Polzin said on Thursday. “We have players who are performing, delivering results, and helping the team succeed. Competition for places does something to a team.”

The coach emphasized that his returning players must earn their opportunities through their performances in training. He also praised the contributions of his substitutes, who have repeatedly influenced matches during HSV’s recent winning streak.

“Everyone is eager and making it very difficult for me to decide who will start,” Polzin said.

HSV’s improving fortunes represent a remarkable reversal from the opening weeks of the campaign. Polzin’s team lost their first three Bundesliga fixtures without scoring a goal, conceding 12 times in defeats against Borussia Dortmund, Mainz 05, and RB Leipzig. Despite the disastrous start, Hamburg’s leadership reaffirmed its faith in Polzin by extending the contracts of the entire coaching staff.

The decision came immediately before the fourth matchday, when HSV finally secured their first victory of the season against 1. FC Köln. A dramatic 3-2 comeback victory away at Hoffenheim followed, before Swiss international Miro Muheim struck another spectacular late winner against Stuttgart.

The improvement has nevertheless come at a significant personnel cost. Remberg sustained a serious knee injury during the Hoffenheim encounter, subsequently underwent surgery for a torn ACL, and faces a lengthy spell on the sidelines. His absence, combined with Sambi Lokonga’s muscular problems, forced Polzin to reconsider his midfield arrangements.

The HSV coach has repeatedly emphasized the importance of tactical flexibility, having already altered his team’s approach several times since assuming responsibility in November 2024. Polzin initially sought to introduce a more progressive possession-based game during the summer, but HSV’s dreadful start prompted a renewed emphasis on defensive stability and compactness.

The effects have been striking. Hamburg averaged only slightly more than 37 percent possession across their recent three-match winning streak, including just 34 percent against Stuttgart. The personnel adjustments have been equally important.

With Remberg and Sambi Lokonga unavailable, Polzin deployed Martin Adeline, Bilal Nadir, and Muheim in an unfamiliar central midfield configuration against Stuttgart. Muheim’s emergence has been particularly significant.

The 28-year-old began the campaign struggling to reclaim his customary position on the left flank following injury problems and increased competition from David Møller Wolfe and Louis Lemke. The Swiss international nevertheless embraced his new central role and has now scored decisive goals in consecutive Bundesliga victories.

His remarkable turnaround has become emblematic of HSV’s collective recovery.

Polzin has also been particularly encouraged by the impact of his substitutes. Against Stuttgart, Albert Grønbæk and Zakaria El Ouahdi entered the match in the 78th minute and combined to create Muheim’s late winner. The coach believes opponents are beginning to recognize the threat HSV pose during the closing stages of matches.

“When the opposing team knows that HSV steps things up towards the end of a match, that’s of incredible value,” Polzin explained.

The returns of Daka, Sambi Lokonga, and Jatta could provide another boost, although Polzin has given no indication that all three will immediately return to the starting XI. Sambi Lokonga offers a potential solution to HSV’s midfield absences, while Daka and Jatta increase the coach’s attacking options.

Saturday’s opponents Paderborn have collected just five points from their opening six Bundesliga fixtures.

By Ellis Stevens | 22 Oct 2026 14:06

The hosts are eighth in the standings with 10 points from nine games, while the visitors are fourth with 14 points after nine league fixtures.

St Mirren head into Saturday's game in desperate search of a streak-snapping victory, with the Saints suffering defeat in all of their last five matches.

Craig McLeish's side have conceded 14 goals and scored just twice during that disastrous run, leading to the pressure rapidly rising on the manager's position at the club.

The downturn in form comes as a stark contrast to St Mirren's start to the season, which saw the Saints win two and draw one of their first three league games, scoring five goals and conceding three goals.

Despite Dundee's struggles at St Mirren's home ground, Pressley's team have enjoyed their most recent clashes with the Saints, winning two and drawing one of their last three encounters.

Tonight’s game against Coventry City has taken on a vital edge. Only six games into the Premier League campaign yet Spurs are winless and propping up the table.

Coventry have won two in a row and no longer look like the whipping boys they did at the start of the season.

Frank Lampard has his own history with Spurs. He did not lose many against them during his stellar playing career with London rivals Chelsea.

Now, he has the Sky Blues up and running and is sure to prey on the anxieties of Spurs at home, where they have won only two league games in the last 12 months.

If De Zerbi’s team look at any stage as if they might fail to beat Coventry, the mood will quickly sour inside the Tottenham Hotspur Stadium.

There were positive signs for Spurs at Manchester United, however, with a spirited fightback and a late equaliser — despite finishing the game with ten men because Rodrigo Bentancur failed the ‘No Red Cards ’til Marbs’ challenge.

De Zerbi claimed afterwards it was the first time he had seen his players show they were ‘a team’ and promised to build on this foundation in Marbella. They flew out after training in London on Tuesday.

De Zerbi values conversations and the bonding experience of a communal meal. He does not usually mind if his players want to drink wine or beer.

He referred to alcohol as ‘good medicine’ at the end of last season when Spurs were fighting relegation and he took them out to high-end restaurant Bacchanalia in Mayfair.

The trip gave De Zerbi more time to work with some of his new signings like Sandro Tonali

There will have been down-time and golf in Marbella but he is unlikely to have let his players out on the lash. De Zerbi’s mind is rarely on anything other than football.

For him, the most critical aspect of these days in Spain will have been to have the luxury of undisturbed time with his players, to work tactically on the grass and to reflect together in meetings and analysis sessions.

Twice during his two seasons at Brighton, he took his players away to Dubai.

First during the World Cup in Qatar in 2022 when the focus was on training rather than bonding. They came home and took 14 points from the next seven games. Spurs would happily sign up for a similar points return if it is on offer.
