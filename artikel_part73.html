#2783. Better Memory Stock: Micron vs. Sandisk

Micron Technology (MU -6.26%) and Sandisk (SNDK -8.51%) are members of an elite club. Only four stocks in the S&P 500 (^GSPC -1.21%) have tripled or more so far in 2026. Micron and Sandisk make up half that group.

Sandisk has been the bigger winner by far this year. But that performance is water under the bridge. Which is the better memory stock to buy now?

Micron and Sandisk are similar in some respects. However, they're two very different kinds of memory stocks.

The most important difference between the two companies is the types of memory they focus on. Micron makes high-bandwidth memory (HBM), dynamic random access memory (DRAM), and NAND memory. Sandisk specializes in NAND flash memory.

HBM is Micron's crown jewel these days. In the company's fiscal 2026 fourth quarter, HBM revenue growth exceeded total revenue growth. Management said that it has completed sales agreements "for the vast majority" of its 2027 calendar-year HBM supply at significantly higher prices than it obtained in 2026.

Sandisk's NAND flash focus has worked to its benefit, though. Surging NAND prices have allowed the company to capture the full upside without any dilution from memory chips that aren't experiencing as strong demand.

But artificial intelligence (AI) serves as a massive tailwind for both companies. Memory is critical to AI applications. In particular, the shift to inference and the rising adoption of agentic AI are driving significant demand for multiple types of memory.

Micron is much bigger than Sandisk. Its market cap tops $1 trillion, making Micron one of the world's top 10 largest technology companies. Meanwhile, Sandisk's market cap is around $200 billion.

Unsurprisingly, Micron also makes more money than Sandisk. In its latest quarter, Micron reported revenue of $54.2 billion and earnings of $37.7 billion. Sandisk's revenue in its latest quarter was $8.97 billion, with earnings of $6.9 billion.

Both companies boast impressive growth. In its fiscal 2026 Q4, Sandisk's revenue soared 372% year over year and 51% quarter over quarter. Micron's fiscal Q4 revenue jumped 379% year over year and roughly 31% quarter over quarter.

Micron and Sandisk are cyclical stocks, which is reflected in their valuations. Micron's shares trade at only seven times forward earnings, while Sandisk's forward earnings multiple is 8x.

Wall Street likes both memory stocks, although the bullish views seem somewhat stronger for Micron. Of the 29 analysts who rated Micron over the past three months, 27 (93%) recommended buying the stock. The consensus 12-month price target for Micron reflects a potential upside of around 74%. Of the 17 analysts who rated Sandisk over the past three months, 15 (88%) recommended buying the stock. The average price target for Sandisk is roughly 60% above the current share price.

Both Micron and Sandisk could (and probably will) continue to deliver exceptional returns over the next few years. But I think that Micron is the better memory stock overall.

While Sandisk's pure-play NAND focus is its greatest strength, it's also the company's most significant risk. If NAND prices fall, Sandisk's revenue and profits will sink. Micron faces risks, too, but its breadth across multiple types of memory should provide some cushion.

I also think that Micron could have a bigger long-term opportunity. In particular, I'm excited about the company's prospects in the humanoid robotics market. Humanoid robots require enormous low-power memory. Micron is investing to meet expected demand and believes it will enjoy "a sustained, substantial multi-decade memory demand cycle" beginning later this decade.

My verdict aligns with Wall Street's take on these two stocks. Micron is the better pick right now.

Though this move won't change the fundamentals or prospects of ASML, it is believed that a lower share price could increase retail demand for the company's stock by making it accessible to a wider pool of investors. Given that ASML has been underperforming the broader semiconductor sector, a stock split could give the stock a much-needed boost.

So, if your brokerage offers the option to buy fractional shares, you can consider investing in ASML regardless of a stock split. That's because the artificial intelligence (AI)-fueled demand for advanced chips is driving strong demand for ASML's chipmaking equipment, leading to a significant acceleration in growth.

However, a significant jump in the number of machines sold by the company last quarter led to a remarkable increase in revenue.

The company expects 14.7 billion euros in revenue in the current quarter, implying a 51% increase over the prior-year period.

Not surprisingly, ASML's consensus earnings per share (EPS) estimates have moved higher.

Even better, analysts have become bullish about ASML's long-term EPS growth prospects.

That's why investors looking to add a top semiconductor stock to their portfolios can consider buying ASML before it steps on the gas as it trades at an attractive 27 times forward earnings right now, a discount to its five-year average forward earnings multiple of 33.

#2760. Better Memory Stock: SK Hynix vs. Micron

The memory market is dominated by three large players: pure-play memory makers SK Hynix (SKHY -8.07%) and Micron Technology (MU -6.26%), as well as the conglomerate Samsung. Combined, the three control over 85% of the DRAM (dynamic random-access memory) market and more than 60% of the NAND (flash) market.

Soaring demand from the artificial intelligence (AI) infrastructure build-out has created a significant supply-demand imbalance in the memory market, which in turn has driven surging memory prices. Much of this stems from the need for high bandwidth memory (HBM), a specialized form of DRAM packaged with graphics processing units (GPUs) and other AI accelerators to reduce latency and ensure these expensive chips aren't left idle due to memory bottlenecks. Demand for HBM is growing in lockstep with demand for compute power, leading the big three memory makers to concentrate their resources on HBM.

However, the ability to increase HBM capacity is limited. ASML is the only company in the world that makes the extreme ultraviolet (EUV) lithography machines needed to manufacture critical HBM components. Meanwhile, these are the same systems needed to make GPUs and other advanced logic chips.

With foundries and memory makers competing for a limited number of new EUV machines, they are unable to keep up with demand. On top of that, HBM requires roughly three times the wafer capacity as ordinary DRAM, adding to the constraints.

With the big three memory makers focused on increasing HBM capacity, the supply-demand balance has also spread to conventional DRAM and NAND. NAND, meanwhile, is seeing huge demand come from the need for massive solid-state drives (SSDs) that store AI training data. As a result, conventional NAND and DRAM prices have risen faster than HBM prices.

This is important when comparing SK Hynix and Micron and determining which looks like the better AI stock to own.

SK Hynix is the market leader in HBM and the memory maker that derives the largest percentage of its revenue from advanced memory. In Q2, it held a 25% share of the DRAM market and a 50% share of the HBM market. It also held a 19% share of the NAND market. The company is the main HBM supplier to Nvidia, and the two companies have a partnership in which Nvidia has locked up $500 billion in future memory commitments.

This compares with Micron, which captured a 24% share of the DRAM market in Q2 but only 18% of the HBM market. Samsung held the other 33% share in the HBM market. Meanwhile, Micron had a 15% share in the NAND market, good for third-highest, right ahead of Japanese company Kioxia and Sandisk. Micron has 26 long-term memory deals in place covering over 35% of its revenue through 2030.

In Q2, 73% of SK Hynix's revenue came from DRAM and 27% from NAND. That was very similar to Micron, which in fiscal Q4 saw 73% of its revenue come from DRAM and 26% from NAND. Both companies are increasing unit production, but the vast majority of growth is due to higher prices.

Since conventional memory prices have been rising faster than HBM prices, Micron has actually seen faster growth. Last quarter, Micron's revenue skyrocketed 379% year over year, while its gross margins expanded from 44.7% to 86.8%. SK Hynix, on the other hand, saw its revenue soar 257%, and its gross margins rise to 83% from 54%.

Both Micron and SK Hynix trade at bargain-basement valuations based on future earnings, as investors fear the end of the memory supercycle. Of the two companies, Micron is more exposed to a drop in conventional memory prices, even with its locked-in long-term commitments, as it derives only a small share of its overall revenue from HBM.

As such, I think that, as the HBM and advanced memory technology leader with strong ties to Nvidia, SK Hynix is ultimately the better memory stock to own over the long term.
